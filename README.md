# layout

View Unity areas and compose Canopy scenes on the phone, in the browser.

    runLayout            # starts it in the background and opens http://localhost:8080
    runLayout stop       # stops it;  runLayout log  follows its log

`runLayout` is a two-line launcher in `~/.local/bin` (on PATH) that runs `bash /sdcard/dev/layout/runLayout.sh "$@"`; scripts on the sdcard cannot run directly, only through `bash`. The launcher and the Termux:Widget button (`~/.shortcuts/runLayout`, same one line) live outside version control: `setup.sh` recreates both. Without them, run `bash /sdcard/dev/layout/runLayout.sh`.

While it runs, any edit to layout's files restarts the server once the files have stopped changing for 1.5 s (so edits in quick succession restart it once, with all of them), and the page reloads itself (or, with unsaved changes, shows Reload). `node server.js` runs it once, without the watcher. The server also watches the Canopy working tree's uncommitted files (those `cache/guid-index.json` marks local, and their folders and every folder above them, up to `Assets`), polling their times each second while a page is open: when one changes (say modl's `python3 obj/curved_bench.py --write` rewrites its benches), it updates the index (rehashing rewritten files in place, else rerunning `tools/guid-index.js`) and the page reloads its scene where the camera is, unless it has unsaved changes. It waits until the files have been quiet a second, so one write reloads once. It knows Tweak: mid-drag the reload waits for the finger to lift, and tweaks not yet written survive it and are put back on the rebuilt pieces. A tweak family's sources (its spec doc, script and library, as its worker names them) are watched too: an edit made outside Tweak, such as a tag changed in Obsidian or the script edited, rebuilds the family from the doc, and the scene reloads. Each request rereads the guid index if the file changed.

Assemblages: a scene whose frontmatter says `assemblage: true` becomes a prefab of its own, `Assets/Assemblages/<Name>/<Name>.prefab` (`tools/assemblage.js`; Name: the scene's name in PascalCase), so another scene places it as one piece, as Unity nests a prefab. Its root is the scene's `- Origin: x y z` line. Pieces whose prefab is a LODGroup over nested models (modl's benches) have those models nested directly under the root's one LODGroup: level k is every such piece's level k, and the last level is one mesh per material combining every piece's last level (`<Name>_Far.obj`), one draw where there were many; Unity's LODGroup only switches renderers and never combines them. Its levels end where the latest piece's own would, plus the assemblage's radius. Other pieces are nested whole, with their own LODs. The server rebuilds an assemblage when its scene is saved and when Canopy files outside `Assets/Assemblages` change (its pieces); only files whose text changed are written. In a scene with an Origin, Tweak keeps a piece its worker anchors on a ring (a curved bench at its row's radius) at its angle around the Origin and moves it to the ring's new radius; leaving Tweak saves the scene with them.

Inspection models: a tool can write OBJ files with a color (`cache/inspect/<Name>.obj` and `.json`: `{color, opacity, material, side}`) for a scene to place as `- Piece: inspect:<Name> | …`. They are drawn lit in their color, both sides unless `side` says `front` or `back` (a back-only copy of a front-only surface lines its inside), see-through below opacity 1 (one naming a Unity material by guid is drawn in that material, textured, while Textures is on), each its own ☰ Folders entry (`Inspect/<Name>`) so layers can be switched on and off. `node tools/mesh-clean.js <Assets/… mesh> [--inspect <Prefix> [--material <name>]] [--skip rejoin] [--write]` (`--material BarkBeech` for the Tangle: its Clean and Original layers textured) makes such layers for a branchy mesh (a tree's uncut collider, e.g. the Tangle's `Tangle_LOD0.mesh`): stray pieces (orphans), its trunk origin, each segment between consecutive rings classed straight, turned, unclear or self-crossing (an hourglass) and whether at the origin, a tip or elsewhere; it rejoins self-crossing segments straight across (`--write` saves the cleaned mesh into Canopy). `--texture-only` (with `--from <local file>` to read another tool's output) only textures every branch afresh, start to end, the cleaner's last step. `node tools/mesh-hub.js <mesh> --radius <units> [--ease <rings>] [--inspect <Prefix>] [--write | --out <file>] [--ball <Assets/... .asset>]` hides a tree's hub, the knot of branch starts at its trunk origin, in a ball, widening the trunk smoothly into it, and writes the ball as a mesh in GridTangle's vertex format (docs scene Tangle Hub). Each run also writes a change log, `cache/mesh-clean/<mesh>.jsonl`: per change, the rule, the branch, the rings, the triangles and why; `node tools/mesh-why.js [<mesh>] [x y z | --probe <n>] [--r <units>]` lists the changes near a point (mesh units; by default the last probe's, `--probe 2` the one before) and the branch and ring it's on; `--probes` lists the recent probes, numbered. The docs scene Inspect Tangle shows them.

Conditions (Ken, 2026-10-29): Canopy switches objects on and off, and moves and scales them, by expressions over its progress flags (`SkyIsle`, `TangleDeposits>=4`, `!Shambles`), evaluated by `MathEval.cs`. ☰ View **Conditions** cycles three ways to draw a scene: **met** (the default: every condition the game can meet, met), **start** (as fast-forwarding to the scene's stage starts it) and **saved** (as saved in Unity, conditions ignored). An object off in the mode is hidden; ☰ View **Inactive** draws it faint instead, its scripts not running. When a scene or prefab is imported, `tools/conditions.js` finds what gates each object, and the gate goes on its piece or part (`when`: terms that must all hold; `never`: off whatever the flags; `moved`: where its triggers put it):
- `ActiveWhen`, `EffectActive` (and `EffectActiveMany`'s targets), and `EffectBoolActive` under an `EffectBoolTrigger`: on exactly while the condition holds.
- Triggers (`OnCondition`, `OnConditionOnce`, `OnConditionDoEffect`, `EffectTrigger`, `EffectConditional`) play the effects on their `effectsToExecute` object: an `EffectScale` or `EffectMove` whose target starts inactive leaves it off until the trigger's condition holds; an `EffectScale` to zero makes it gone then (the Sky Isle's cloud); `EffectMove`, `EffectTransform` and `EffectScale` to a size move it (`moved`); a Pod's `Revealer` (PodRequest) hides the pieces under its `revealRoot` until then.
- `HideOnStart`, a disabled renderer, and an object saved inactive that nothing switches on: never on in the game (the Sky Isle's guard Blowtorches, the four FruitLoci).

A scene's symbols (each flag's value in met and start) come with it from the server: met has Game.unity's `FastForwardSymbols` for every stage, then every flag an `EffectSetProgressFlag` or `EffectMath` anywhere in Canopy sets (set to one: 1; incremented: how many do), and each stage's `<Stage>Deposits` at its Gates' most required; start has only the stages before the scene's. Both have `Stage` and `StageName` from the scene's name (`TangleScene`: 2, Tangle). Flags code sets at run time (`Underwater`, `RingsWorn`) are unset unless `unity.json` `symbols` sets them; a composed scene's `- Symbols: A=1 B=0` lines win. `web/conditions.js` is MathEval ported with its quirks (an unset flag is null, so even `X==0` fails until something sets X), and with Canopy's two fixes (2026-10-29: every waiting operator of equal or higher priority goes first, so `1-2*3+4` is -1; a `!` pops nothing, so `!!a` works). Like Canopy's MathEval it compiles each condition once (`compile`, `run`): the page evaluates programs. `Conditions.test()` runs MathEval's own test cases, both ways. The compiled design was proven here, against the interpreter on 340,000 random expressions and flag tables, before it went into Canopy. Not read yet: `FilterWithStageData` (which some ActiveWhens also test; no scene so far has one), an instance's override of a condition inside its prefab, and conditions on stand-in scripts (a gated particle system plays as saved).

Gems (Ken, 2026-10-29): a gem isn't saved in the scene; as each stage starts, its `GemLocus` makes it, so an imported area does too (`tools/gems.js`). `GemTypes` (`Prefabs/Shared/GemTypes.prefab`, with `Game.unity`'s added types) gives the locus's ability (`abilityId`, the scene's override) a shape and a color; the shape's gem prefab goes at the locus's `gemHolder`, keeping its own rotation (relative to the locus) and its own world size, as `Make.cs` places it; everything under its `model` takes the color's material, and its breadcrumbs (`BreadcrumbGemPfx`, the effect around a gem on its locus) the color's color. Its puisance shows only for a gem locked in place, and its glow and pick effects only on events, so they don't come. `tools/unity.js` reads `GemLocus`'s and `Gem`'s fields for this without running them (a graph's `read`).

Animation: `web/ticker.js` is the page's clock. Each frame the render loop calls `Ticker.tick(dt)` (seconds since the last frame, at most 0.1; 0 while ☰ **Tick** is off, which freezes everything animated) and it calls every function added with `Ticker.add((dt, time) => …)` (which returns a function that removes it); `time` is the sum of the dts, standing still while the page is hidden, and is also the shaders' `canopyTime`. A function that throws is dropped and its error shown. Scripts run on it.

Scripts (Ken, 2026-10-29): code attached to a piece, as a MonoBehaviour is to a Unity game object, run on the Ticker while the piece is in the scene: a class derived from `Tickable` (`web/tickable.js`, which says what each has: its piece, params, the camera, the environment, and for stand-ins their saved fields), in a file named for it that calls `Tickable.register("<Name>", class extends Tickable { start() {} tick(dt) {} stop() {} })`. They come two ways. Layout's own prefabs, `prefabs/<Name>.md`, for what Canopy lacks or layout runs its own way: `- Shape:` lines as a docs asset's (`use X.prefab` nests that Unity prefab, even where a layout prefab shares its name) and `- Script: <file> | settings` lines; a piece names one like any prefab, layout's winning over a Unity prefab of the same name, and they are never exported to Unity. And the C# scripts of Unity prefabs: one with a JS stand-in, `scripts/<ClassName>.js`, runs with its saved fields (`tools/unity.js` reads them, and the prefab's objects, which a stand-in may move); the rest are only named, and the probe report lists a piece's scripts (`(no stand-in)` for those). A prefab instance's overrides of a stand-in's settings (a C# script's fields, a particle system's and its renderer's properties) are applied through every level of nesting, as Unity does, except those naming objects (they would need their ids translated from file to file). A script's params are its class's static ones, then its Script line's settings, then its piece's (`rate 2, speed 4`: numbers or lists of them). A class with `static singleton = true` runs once: a second piece with it is hidden and its scripts stopped (the first loaded wins, and a scene's included pieces load before its own). Editing a script, a stand-in or a layout prefab restarts the server, and the page reloads.

- **Sea** (`prefabs/Sea.md`): a cube nesting Canopy's own `Sea.prefab`, drawn and run as the game does: stand-ins for its `Sea.cs` (a singleton: the waterline's height and how things darken below it, from its `dimAmountDefault` and `dimDepth`), `Abyss.cs` (the prefab at the world's origin, its altitude from SeaCanonicalAltitude, 135, or a scene's `environment.seaLevel`, as a biome's local altitude; the water kept under the camera in its 14 by 12 m steps, moved only when looking steeply up from above the sea or down from below, or ten steps off) and `WaterScroller.cs` (each surface's two scrolls drifting by its `flow1` and `flow2`). Its two surfaces are drawn by their shaders' names (`web/surface.js` byShader, which also draws Canopy/Water, the bubbles' and the Mega's shield's, Canopy/Force Field, the cages' spheres, Canopy/CloudTextured, the clouds, each as its shader does, Canopy/DiffuseGlow, the mana fire on flamers (unlit, its bright texels blazing), and Canopy/Goop as its surface does, lit simply where Unity uses Standard lighting): Canopy/SeaSurface as the game's, on the mesh's own UVs and its material's `_Normal1` and `_Normal2` tilings; the underside as Canopy/WaterBelow, a cheap shader of Canopy's own since 2026-10-29 (ambient light on its color, a Fresnel sheen of the water's color, fog at `_FogLevel`; it was Unity's Standard lighting). The surfaces leave the piece for the world (so they aren't picked up, snapped to or framed); tap the cube.
- **Sky** (`prefabs/Sky.md`, `scripts/Skybox.js`, a singleton): CanSky on a dome round the camera, from the environment's `sky` and sky settings, as Game.unity's skybox with EnvironmentManager's sky globals. Without one the background is plain.
- **Sun** (`prefabs/Sun.md`, `scripts/Sun.js`, a singleton; Ken, 2026-11-01): the scene's one light, Canopy's sun from the environment's `sunDirection`, `sunColor` and `ambient`. Canopy's ported shaders take these anyway; the Sun lights Three.js's own materials the same way (a directional light and an ambient one, each pi strong, as Three.js's Lambert divides by pi), and turns off the page's plain lights (a hemisphere light and a fixed sun), which light scenes without one.
- **Wind** (`prefabs/Wind.md`, `pfx/Wind.js`, a singleton; an experiment, Canopy has no wind yet): very light tan lines wafting along +X, their front 15% (by length, `headFade`) fading in from near transparent and rounded, widening from nothing as a quarter ellipse, drawn with eight points of its own (`nosePoints`), so the leading edge is soft (2026-10-30), each a chain of points whose head alone moves, weaving gently (at most 0.2 m up or down per meter), laying points that stay put while the tail eats the oldest; each starts to the -X side of the camera within 3 m of its height (never lower than 3 m over the sea), placed to cross the view, and is gone in about ten seconds; it blows in gusts, lines starting for ten seconds, then none for five to ten.
- **Spinner** (`scripts/Spinner.js`, for Canopy's `Spinner.cs`): turns its `partToSpin` (else its own object) about its axis once every `secondsToSpin`, from a random start, as `Transform.Rotate` does. Stand-ins move and turn a prefab's objects with `offset` and `turn` (`web/tickable.js`). The docs scene Harvesters shows every harvester, spinners spinning.
- **Flamer and HcFaceTarget** (`scripts/Flamer.js`, `scripts/HcFaceTarget.js`): a flamer (Flamer, Blowtorch) with no target, as the game shows it: HcFaceTarget turns the head toward Flamer's aim, at most `rotationSpeed` a second, and with no target that aim is `straightUp`, which rides on the flamer's Spinner, so the head sweeps round; only within `scanRange` (40 m) of the camera. Stand-ins can also aim an object (`orient`) and read where a moved one is now (`positionOf`, `rotationOf`).
- **Particle systems** (`scripts/ParticleSystem.js`, from `tools/particles.js`, which reads a ParticleSystem and its renderer into a small description; Ken, 2026-10-29): Unity's Shuriken systems that play on awake, after the overrides of every prefab instance they're in (the rest wait for game events layout doesn't run, and aren't sent; HarvesterJet, in every flamer, turns its steam's off, so only a douse plays it): emission (rate and bursts) from the shape (cone, sphere, hemisphere, box, circle, edge), start lifetime, speed, size, rotation and color, then size, rotation, color and velocity over each particle's life, gravity, noise, local or world space, a texture sheet, drawn as camera-facing billboards in one batch by a port of the material's shader (Canopy/ParticleContrast, ParticleGeneral, ParticleSolid; Canopy/Blank draws nothing; others as texture times color; Unity's Default-Particle as a soft dot). Particle colors reach the shader as Unity hands them: converted to linear (Canopy draws in linear) only where the renderer's Apply Active Color Space is on; most of Canopy's have it off, so their colors go unconverted and look stronger (2026-10-30: the gems' green showed white). Unity blends in linear and the page in sRGB, so additive glows still differ a little in how they fall off. Noise (2026-10-29: the gems' breadcrumbs wander) moves particles as a velocity from a gradient noise field (Ken Perlin's improved noise) at each particle's position: each axis its own field, sampled at the position times the frequency, moved along by the scroll speed, octaves added, in 1D, 2D or 3D by its quality, times the axis's strength over the particle's life (divided by the frequency with damping, as Unity's docs describe it) and the position amount; its remap and its effect on rotation and size aren't done (Canopy uses neither). Unity's noise function isn't public, so the field differs from the game's in detail, not in strength or scale. Modules it doesn't do (trails, collision, stretched billboards...) are named once each in the console. As in the game, a system in a LODGroup level runs and shows only while that level is drawn (the page's LOD pass keeps each piece's chosen levels for scripts), and **PfxLod** (`scripts/PfxLod.js`) thins its emission with the camera's distance every 5 s and pauses it below 0.4 a second; off screen (the box round its particles, as of its last update, out of the camera's view) it does what its culling mode says, as Unity does: Automatic pauses a looping system and keeps simulating any other, Pause pauses, Pause and Catch-up catches up the missed time (up to its duration) when seen again, Always Simulate keeps simulating unseen (in the Tangle 273 of 276 pause); and a system more than 100 m from the camera waits. The Driller's smoke (DrillerSmoke) was the first.
- **Cage** (`scripts/Cage.js`, for Canopy's `Cage.cs`): its sphere drawn in `liveMat` while anything guards it, else `deadMat`, as `AdjustMat` does. Guards are counted when a Canopy scene is imported: each active instance whose `thingGuarded` (HcGuard's) names the cage, as HcGuard registers when the scene starts; the piece says `guards N`.

The docs scene World holds them (and LeafGust); `unity.json`'s `include` puts it in every scene from Canopy (an imported area, or a scene on one), and any other scene takes it with `- Include: World`. A scene without a Sea has no sea and nothing darkens; without a Sky, no sky.

Effects (pfx): `web/pfx.js`'s `Pfx` is a Tickable that is a tiny particle system whose effects are code: `pfx/<Name>.js` registers a class derived from it, with `spawn(p)`, `update(p, dt)` and `remove(p)` (and `setup`, `dispose`), each optional (`web/pfx.js` says what each gets). Particles spawn at `rate` a second, at most `max` at once, each living `life` seconds unless `update` ends it sooner. An effect works in Unity coordinates: its piece's own (scaled with it), or with `static space = "world"` the world's, the piece only switching it on. It has `space` (the group for its meshes), `eye` (the camera, in those coordinates), `inView(near, far)` (a random point the camera sees) and `ribbon(n, options)` (a strip of n points facing the camera, a line with thickness, set each frame with a width and an alpha per point). A layout prefab's Script line attaches it.

A LODGroup may name renderers inside nested prefabs or models (stripped renderers), as modl's prefabs do (a LODGroup over nested `<Name>_LOD<k>.obj` models): those models' meshes take its levels.

The reticle button (right of ⛶) probes: a ray from the camera through the screen's center; the first visible piece it hits is marked with a yellow dot and reported on the status line, and the server keeps the full report in `cache/probes.jsonl` (newest last) and logs it: the piece, its model, object, material and LOD levels, the point in Unity world and piece-local coordinates, the surface normal, the distance, the hit triangle's corners and the 6 vertices nearest the hit (piece-local, with their distance in metres), and the camera's place and direction, and its scripts, if it has any. On a `mesh-clean.js` layer (its `.json` names its `source` mesh) the server adds, a few seconds later, `branch`: the branch and ring the point is on and the changes near it (`tools/mesh-why.js --json`). Tell Claude "what about this?" and it reads the latest. Holding the button 1.2 s goes back to where the latest probe was taken (from any page), facing the same way, and shows its dot.

☰ Inspect layers (shown when the scene has inspection models) lists each layer with its color and an on/off box, leaving out layers with nothing in them (say, no orphans left). Both lists scroll when they outgrow the screen. A scene line `- Switch: Before = A B | After = C` adds a top-bar button naming the set of inspection layers now shown; a tap shows the next set alone (its layers on, the other sets' off), for a before and after in the same place (docs scene Compare Tangle: `mesh-clean.js` writes `<Prefix>Changed`, every triangle it changes, red, to lay over Original). What ☰ Folders and ☰ Inspect layers switch off is kept per scene in the browser between runs.

☰ Origin shows a scene's `- Origin` (an assemblage's root) as axes drawn through everything: red Unity +X, green +Y, blue +Z (0 degrees); it is greyed out in scenes without one.

☰ Reference cube shows a 1 m cube to judge sizes by: tap it to carry it, then Drop or Cancel. Where it is, per scene, is kept in the browser (local storage) and never saved in the scene; the first time it sits where the reticle points.

Add lists Pieces (one piece each: modl's models first, then new assets, reuses and Unity prefabs) or Scenes (assemblage scenes, added whole as their prefab; the open scene is left out); the choice is remembered. A piece from an included assemblage moves the whole assemblage; to move its pieces, open the assemblage's own scene. An added modl piece brings its LODs (its prefab's LODGroup) and its tweakvars.

Tweak (the sliders button right of ⛶, both at the right end of the top bar; shown when the scene has pieces a tweak worker serves: `unity.json` `tweakWorkers`, e.g. modl's `obj/serve.py` for `Assets/Modl/<Family>/` prefabs) reshapes them live. Its list holds the family's tweakvars (the `{name value unit}` tags of the model's `# Modl` section in the docs), alphabetical, its scroll position and the selected var kept in the browser like Snap. Tap a var, then drag left and right: across the screen spans its range (at most twice its value); geometry follows the finger, each LOD level's model and the LODGroup's switch heights with it (the worker answers in tens of milliseconds; one request at a time, the latest winning), textures are redone when the finger lifts. A var the worker marks rebuild asks for a number and rebuilds at once. Tap the selected var again to look around. While tweaking, Add, Drop, Cancel and Delete hide and undo and redo step through tweaks. Leaving Tweak writes the values into the doc's tags and the full build into Canopy (the worker's `write`), and the scene reloads. Workers start on first use and stop after ten idle minutes.

☰ holds New, Save, Import area, Folders, Inspect layers, Snap, Reference cube and **View ▸**, which holds the view toggles (Textures, Labels, Wireframe, LOD tint, Fog, Fog far clip, Culling, Origin, Candidate trees, Tick) and stays open while they are flipped; below, ☰ Labels and the like are in it.

Tap ⛶ for full screen, or add the page to the home screen.

Errors fail loudly: any page error shows in red in the status line and is written to `layout.log` (`runLayout log`), next to the server's own messages.

Load times go to `layout.log` too (`grep load: layout.log`): each scene load (and each Add that prepares a new piece) writes the server's time, split into its slow steps (reading docs, prefabs, git, converting meshes and textures, scanning the Canopy working tree for changed textures) with counts; then the page writes its own: the scene's JSON, then its meshes and textures, with how many it downloaded.

## Two kinds of scene

- **Composed scenes** are yours: `docs/scenes/<Type> <Name>.md` (e.g. `Landmark Ring Forge.md`), one Piece line per piece. Create one with ☰ **New**; edit it here or by hand. They are what will be exported to Unity. A line `- Include: <Scene>` draws another composed scene's pieces beneath, read-only, their scripts running (World: the sea, sky and wind); included scenes' own includes come too, each scene once, and their Area lines are ignored. Include lines aren't exported.
- **Imported areas** are read-only copies of a cube of a Unity scene (TangleScene Whole is all of the Tangle's, every piece with its own LODs from Canopy): ☰ **Import area** (or `node tools/scene.js extract <Scene> <cx> <cy> <cz> <halfSize> <Name>`) writes `areas/<Scene> <Name>.json`. Regenerate them whenever Unity changes. Its Terrain only box brings just the scene's trees, none of its gameplay pieces (e.g. the Tangle's GridTangle alone). Trees (already cut into cubic chunks, `Meshes/Grid*`) come in as one read-only piece per tree holding only its chunks whose bounds overlap the cube, every LOD level of each (see LODs). Chunk bounds are read from each chunk's smallest LOD and cached. A tree's leaves (one mesh for the whole tree) come too, cut to the cube; scenes save their trees with leaves switched off (turned on at run time), so a show rule in `unity.json` brings the leaves from the tree prefab and the piece says so. Cutout materials (a shader that clip()s, e.g. Canopy/LeafCut, or Standard Cutout) are drawn with alpha testing.

## unity.json

What an imported area shows is set in `unity.json`, not in code: `defaults`, plus a section per scene under `scenes` (its values replace the defaults; its show and hide lists add to them).

- `near`, `far`: the camera's clip planes in meters, the game's by default (0.03 and 400: `Game.unity`'s OVRCameraRig camera, which both eyes use; `FarClipManager` may pull far in at run time, to 40 m in clouds). Nothing past `far` is drawn, as in the game; raise it to see farther while composing.
- `gridDrawsInactiveParts` (true): draw a GridRender tree's chunks even though its `Parts` object is inactive, as GridRender does (scenes save it inactive; GridRender skips only disabled renderers). `gridLodByDistance` (true): pick GridRender chunk levels by distance, as Canopy's GridRender does since 2026-10-25 (it measured a vertical segment's screen height, which shrinks seen from above); false shows the old way.
- `leafThinning`: LeafSplit's thinning to preview where a scene's LeafSplit has none: `{ "start": 40, "end": 200, "keep": 0.3, "grow": true }` (meters, the share left at `end`, grow the rest); `keep` 1 is off.
- `environment.leafWind`: LeafSplit's leaves in the wind, Canopy's `iLeafCutWind` shader's settings with its defaults, to tune here and then set on the material: `{ "sway": 0.6, "flutter": 0.15, "frequency": 1.5, "gustLength": 12, "gustSpeed": 4, "gustShape": 2, "near": 0.25, "nearDistance": 8, "fadeDistance": 1000 }`. Blown, each leaf turns rigidly about its stem toward its own axis plus `sway` of downwind (1 turns a leaf across the wind 45°) and `flutter` of its face's direction, back and forth `frequency` times a second, at wind strength 1 (medium), the steady wind and gusts added. The wave gusts (☰ View Gusts as waves) are waves `gustLength` m apart moving downwind at `gustSpeed` m/s, lulling to stillness between them (the wave from 0 to 1 raised to `gustShape`: higher, shorter gusts and longer calms). Only `near` of the motion at the eye, all of it from `nearDistance` m (calm close up, against VR sickness); none beyond `fadeDistance` m (full to half of it). Only the fields given change. Drawn in the vertex shader (`web/app.js` windPatch), as the game's shader does; the stem and tip come from the leaf's uv.y (0 and 1), as LeafSplit places its leaves.
- The wind itself is LeafGust's (Ken, 2026-10-31): World's LeafGust piece (`prefabs/LeafGust.md`, run by `scripts/LeafGust.js`, the stand-in for Canopy's `LeafGust.cs`). A steady wind everywhere plus gusts, each a level, added, never past strong: ☰ View **Wind** cycles the steady wind (still, light, medium, strong: strengths 0, 0.4, 0.7, 1) and **Gusts** the gusts (none, light, medium, strong: 0, 0.4, 1, 1.7); **Gusts as** switches cylinders and waves; remembered per scene. Cylinder gusts: up to three round columns of wind at once, never more, each passing through a point in front of the camera (`nearest` 10 to `farthest` 60 m away, within `viewAngle` 40° of where it faces), starting `approach` (25 m) upwind of it and ending as far downwind, building up and dying out, so it is strongest there; heading along `direction` (+X, a little +Z) give or take `directionJitter` (15°); `radius` 25 m (light 15, strong 35), easing off over the last `edge` (4 m), at `speed` 4 m/s; the next at least `interval` (4 s, give or take `intervalJitter`, half) later and in a free slot. Light gusts are smaller, slower (0.75) and rarer (1.4 times the interval); strong ones bigger, faster (1.5) and more often (0.7). A leaf whose stem is in a gust (tested by squared distance: no square roots) is blown along its heading; it takes the strongest it's in. Settings on World's LeafGust line change these (e.g. `radius 30, interval 6`). The Wind effect's lines follow it (`pfx/Wind.js`): a few for the steady wind, along it, and more inside each gust, travelling with it.
- `lodFov`: the headset's vertical field of view per eye, in degrees, used to pick LOD levels as the game would (90 for now; to be measured in the game, `Camera.fieldOfView` under XR). `lodBias`: Unity's LOD bias (QualitySettings; Android's level has 1). `treeChunks`: path pattern of chunk meshes (`Meshes/Grid*`).
- `maxTexture`: largest texture side kept, in pixels (1024). A texture's alpha is what Unity's import makes it (its `.meta`'s Alpha Source, which `tools/guid-index.js` notes): None drops it, From Gray Scale makes it the average of R, G and B (the gems' glow, `VFX_Glow.png`, is an RGB image whose black ground is clear that way), and Input Texture Alpha keeps its own (TIF and PSD extra channels count as alpha). Converted textures are cached by source, size and alpha source.
- `show`: rules forcing objects on even when the scene or prefab switches them off (the tree leaves: `mesh:ForestTreeLeaves*`).
- `hide`: rules removing objects.
- `symbols`: progress flags code sets at run time, for ☰ View Conditions (see Conditions): `{ "Underwater": 0 }`.
- `include`: the composed scenes every scene from Canopy includes, as an `- Include:` line would (an imported area, or a scene on one): `["World"]`.
- `environment`: the sun and fog Canopy's shaders get (`ShaderVars.cs`): `sunDirection` (Unity world, toward the sun), `sunColor`, `ambient` (Canopy's ambient intensity, 0 to 1), `fogColor`, `fogDensity` (the game's least is 0.014, `fogDensityMinimum` in `Game.unity`); the sky, `sky` (a material name: `CanSky`, the skybox) and its settings (below); and `seaLevel`, null where the Sea prefab's own altitude stands (135), set where a scene's biome sets its own (TutorialScene's 200.48). The sea is the Sea prefab's (its values are `Sea.prefab`'s). The game sets the light per biome and mood at run time, so those are stand-ins; set them per scene to match. ☰ **Fog** turns the fog off.

A rule is `kind:pattern`, `*` matching anything: `prefab:Cairn` (a whole piece), `prefab:Cairn/PlaceholderObject` (an object inside it), `object:`, `mesh:`, `material:`, `folder:Prefabs/PFX`. Example: `"scenes": {"BeechScene": {"hide": ["folder:Prefabs/PFX"]}}`. An area is re-imported automatically the next time it is opened after a `unity.json` change to what it bakes in: `show`, `hide`, `treeChunks`, `gridDrawsInactiveParts`, `gridLodByDistance` and `leafThinning`, as they come out for its Unity scene; or when a stand-in (`scripts/`) is added or removed, since an area keeps its pieces' stand-in scripts (their saved fields; editing one needs no re-import); or when how prefabs are read changes (`tools/unity.js` READER). The rest (clip planes, culling, LOD, environment, include and so on) is read each time a scene loads and needs no re-import.

A composed scene can be built on an imported area: a line `- Area: <Scene> <Name>` (e.g. `- Area: BeechScene Rings`) draws that area beneath it, read-only. Its pieces can't be picked up, but Snap rests pieces on them, and Folders and Labels cover them. Save writes only the scene's own Piece lines; export will take only those. While viewing an area, **Add** (or ☰ **New**) asks for a scene name, makes the scene on that area, and keeps the camera where it is.

The Add list has three sections: new assets from docs (◇ drawn from shapes, ▢ a labeled box), reused assets by docs name (`Tablet → RuinTablet`, from 🟢 Asset lines; placed as the prefab), and every Unity prefab, so any Unity object can be placed.

Placeholder shapes: a new asset with `- Shape:` lines indented under its docs Asset line is drawn from them (◇ in the Add list) instead of a labeled box (▢). Shapes: `box w h d`, `cylinder r h`, `cone r h`, `pyramid w h d` (a w by d base), `sphere r`, `hemisphere r` (flat side down), `dodecahedron size`, `octahedron size` (point to point, a point up; `octahedron 0.5 scale 1 2 1` is 1 m tall), `icosahedron size`, `ico60 size` (a triakis icosahedron: an icosahedron with a low pyramid on each face, 60 faces; point to point, an edge up, so `ico60 0.6` is 0.51 m tall), `prism w h d` (a triangle w wide and h tall, extruded d thick), `use <Name>` (another asset's shapes, or a Unity prefab, e.g. `use Wood boards at 0 1 0`); then optionally `at x y z`, `turn rx ry rz` (degrees), `color #rrggbb`, `material <Name>` (a Unity material by file name, one word, e.g. `material ModlMana`; drawn with it here and used by the placeholder prefab; the color stays as a fallback), `sides N` (cylinder, cone), `tip f` (cone, pyramid: cut off flat, the top f times the base, a snub nose), `scale x y z`, `arc degrees` (hemisphere: keep a wedge, e.g. `arc 90`); `use <Name> color #rrggbb` recolors all of it. Meters, Unity axes; each shape rests on its origin. Bad lines show as errors. Reload the page after editing them.

Unity names: a docs asset's prefab is its name in PascalCase (Burl shard -> `BurlShard`), or the name after `as` on its Asset line (`- Asset: 🟢 Model: Command Aerie as Lookout`); `as LeafSingle.fbx` names a model file, placed whole (also in `use`). When that prefab exists in the project, layout draws it instead of the shapes.

Placeholder prefabs: `node tools/make-shapes.js` lists, and `--write` writes, Unity files for every asset with shapes and no prefab yet, all under `Assets/Placeholder` in the Canopy working tree: `<Name>.prefab` (an empty root holding the models and any `use` prefabs), `Models/<Name>.obj` (one per color), and `Materials/Shape_<rrggbb>.mat` (`Canopy/General`, the color as its Tint). Guids come from file paths, so reruns keep references. The folder is the script's: move a prefab out before customising it. A `use ... color` on a Unity prefab isn't applied there; the script says so. Unverified in Unity until it is next opened.

All new assets at once: `node tools/new-assets-scene.js` writes `docs/scenes/New Assets.md`, every 🔴 and 🟡 docs asset in rows 15 m above the sea (🔴 in front, by type then name), spaced by size. Rerun it after Asset or Shape lines change.

Piece line: `- Piece: Name | x y z | rx ry rz | scale | extra settings`, in Unity's world coordinates and euler degrees. The name is a Unity prefab, a mesh, or a new asset from a docs Asset line (shown as a labeled box until it has a model). Settings are comma-separated: `textId <id>` (a tablet's text), `material <Name>` (the whole piece drawn in that material, its own ignored; docs scene Tangle Bone).

Layout's own materials: `materials/<Name>.json` (`map` and `normal`, image files beside it; optional `color` [r, g, b, a] and `scale` [x, y]) is a material for previewing what is not in Canopy yet, drawn as Canopy's plain lit materials are (no tint unless `color` says). A `material <Name>` setting takes it before a Unity material of that name. Bone (`materials/Bone.json`) was made from a photo: `node tools/tile-texture.js <photo> <tile>` makes it seamless (each edge overlapped onto the opposite one and joined along the cut where they differ least, so holes aren't ghosted; it loses the overlap, about a fifth of each side), then `node tools/texture-maps.js <tile> 1024 materials Bone --depth 8 [--preview <file>]` writes `BoneA.png` (the albedo: the photo's baked shading low frequencies removed and the rest kept at 0.6, scaled to mean luma 150) and `BoneN.png` (a tangent-space normal map, OpenGL's way as Unity wants, from the brightness taken as height; `--depth` is its relief), both still tiling; `--preview` draws them lit to check.

## LODs

Layout draws LOD levels as the game picks them, every frame, as seen by a camera with the headset's field of view (`lodFov`) from where Layout's camera is:

- **LODGroups** (prefabs' and nested prefabs' own, in areas and composed scenes): Unity's rule. The group's size (times its object's scale) over its distance from the camera, as a share of the view's height (size / (2 · distance · tan(fov/2)), times `lodBias`); the first level whose screen height that reaches is drawn, else nothing.
- **Trees a GridRender draws** (`StdTreeLods`: Beech, Oak, Maple, the tutorial tree; the Tangle, whose scene adds one): Canopy's `GridRender.TestLoDs`. Per chunk: if the box of all its level meshes is in view (as Canopy's GridRender does since 2026-10-26; it tested a cube `objectSize` times the tree's scale across at the chunk's part, which missed geometry the slicer left outside it, so branches vanished while their part's centre was behind the player), the cube's size over its distance as a share of the view's height, in percent (`gridLodByDistance`; before, the screen height between its bottom and top centers, which shrinks seen from above); the first of GridGroup's `cutoffArray` it reaches is drawn, else nothing. The chunks' own LODGroups are ignored, as in the game, where GridRender switches them off.
- **Other gridded trees** (the mushroom): their chunks' LODGroups, resized as `GridGroup.AdjustCutoffs` does at startup (size `objectSize`, heights the cutoffs when it has them).
- A tree's leaves use their own LODGroup (`TreeLeavesLods`), except where the scene adds a LeafSplit to the tree (the Beech, Oak and Maple scenes): the game then draws every leaf itself, instanced, out to its `maxRenderDistance` (250 m), so the most detailed leaf mesh comes, cut to the area leaf by leaf, and is drawn as LeafSplit would, with its thinning: farther leaves drop out a share at a time (each leaf has a fixed random rank, the same hash as `LeafSplit.LeafRank`; at distance d a leaf shows while its rank is under keep(d), from 1 at `start` to `keep` at `end`), the rest grown by 1/√keep with `grow`; a leaf shrinks to nothing over the last `fade` (5%) of ranks, and every leaf over the last 10% of the distance limit, instead of blinking out. The scene's LeafSplit settings are used when it has thinning on (`thinKeep` under 1); else `unity.json`'s `leafThinning` previews it (`keep` 1 turns the preview off). The piece's settings line says which.
- **Candidate trees**: chunk meshes a tool wrote to `cache/candidates/` (the same paths as in Canopy; `node tools/dice.js --candidate`) are paired with the shipped ones when a scene loads; ☰ **Candidate trees** (shown when there are any) switches every tree between the two, with the same LODs, so ☰ **LOD tint**'s triangle count compares them. A candidate tree's own GridRender cutoffs (`cache/candidates/<prefab>.lod.json`, from `tree-build.js`) apply with it. Nothing goes into Canopy until a tool's `--write`.
- Not yet: LODGroups on plain scene objects (outside prefabs); those show their most detailed level.

## Composing

- **Add** opens a filterable list (new assets from docs, then Unity prefabs). The piece appears on the cursor a few meters ahead, sized to about a quarter of the screen.
- Drive the camera like a player with the WASD pad (W toward where the camera points, S straight back, A and D sidestep, Q down, E up), or the same keys on a keyboard. One finger looks around (carrying a piece, a sideways drag circles the camera around it instead); a pinch travels forward and back like W and S. over 2 s of holding, every move key (W, S, A, D, Q, E) speeds up from a third of the speed to twenty times it, A and D at half that; a pinch goes farther the faster the fingers move (four times 1 + (speed / 400 px a second)^1.5, that factor at most 12), so slow pinches stay fine.
- While a piece is carried it keeps its place relative to the camera, so moving carries it. Dragging circles the camera around it, leaving it where it is (a screen-wide drag is a quarter turn); A and D spin the piece itself, a quarter turn a second.
- ☰ **Snap** (toggle) rests the carried piece on whatever is below it.
- ☰ **Labels** (toggle) names every piece just above it; placeholder boxes are always named.
- Each scene remembers its own camera place and direction (brought back within 100 m, or half the scene's size, of what it draws if it was further off), **Textures**, ☰ **Snap**, ☰ **Labels**, ☰ **Fog**, ☰ **LOD tint**, ☰ **Wireframe** and ☰ **Candidate trees** (in this browser); a scene not opened before is framed whole, with Textures and Fog on and the rest off.
- ☰ **Fog far clip** sets the far clip as the game's FarClipManager intends: where Canopy's fog hides 95% of the light (`unity.json` `farClip`: threshold 0.95, min 10, max 400, from `Game.unity`; 148 m at density 0.014), with the sky's radius following it; off, the fixed `far`. (Canopy's own FarClipManager reads a fog density that is never set, and a plain exponential, so in the game it stays at 400 m for now.)
- ☰ **Culling** stands in for Unity's CullingGroup (Unity 5 on; Unity works out distance bands itself and calls back on changes): each piece has a cull distance (`unity.json` `culling` rules, first match, e.g. trees 400 m since they show against the sky; else `"fog"`, where the fog hides 95%); each frame `perFrame` (5%) of the pieces is checked in turn, and one is hidden once its nearest point is past its distance plus `margin` (6 m), shown again once back inside. ☰ LOD tint's legend counts the pieces culled.
- ☰ **Wireframe** draws every visible triangle's edges over the lit surfaces, to see how a mesh is built (kept per scene, like the other toggles).
- ☰ **LOD tint** colors each mesh by the LOD level drawn (L0 green, L1 yellow, L2 orange, L3 red, L4 magenta, L5 blue, L6 cyan); the legend, top right, counts the triangles drawn.
- The scale bar sets its size as a multiple of its base size, ×0.25 to ×49, each step a fixed percentage (it shows the piece's size when picked up).
- **Drop** places it; **Cancel** puts it back (or discards a new one) and returns the camera to where the carry began; **Delete** removes a placed piece. These show only while carrying.
- Tap a placed piece to pick it up again. **Undo** and **Redo** cover drops and deletes. ☰ **Save** writes the Piece lines (☰ shows ● while there are unsaved changes).
- Tablets, signs and notes (prefabs with a script that has a `textId` field) show a text from the game's `Assets/Resources/Text/English.txt`. While carrying one, **Add text** lists its text ids by section with each one's first line; pick one, or type an id that isn't there yet and use it. Drop keeps it, as `textId <id>` in the Piece line. Docs Text assets are not in the Add list.
- ☰ **Folders** hides pieces by prefab folder (Items, Harvesters, …).

## How it gets meshes

The browser keeps every file the server sends (meshes, textures, scripts, the page), checking each time: a file comes with an ETag of its size and modification time, and one unchanged since comes back as a 304, without being read again (2026-10-31: Oak's 1345 meshes and textures, 89 MB, in 1.1 s instead of 2.8 s). A rebuilt or edited file is sent again.

The server resolves each piece name through its Unity prefab (nested prefabs and variants followed; every LOD level a LODGroup draws, else the most detailed of sibling LOD meshes; only meshes with an enabled renderer), fetches the `.asset` meshes it needs from the Canopy git repo (through a throwaway store, `~/.cache/layout-fetch-<pid>.git`, one per process, deleted after each fetch, so Canopy's own git store stays lean; blobs Canopy already has are read from it), and converts them to OBJ in `cache/meshes/`. `node tools/guid-index.js` rebuilds `cache/guid-index.json` after the Canopy repo changes, including uncommitted changes in the working tree (new, changed, renamed or deleted assets), whose files are then read from the working tree. The committed part is kept per commit (`cache/guid-index-head.json`), so a rerun on the same commit only rereads the working tree.

Draw order follows Unity's render queues (2026-10-30): each material's queue is its own (`m_CustomRenderQueue`) or else its shader's `Queue` tag (`Transparent+1` and the like), and a mesh or particle system with a queue past 2500 is drawn in queue order before distance (three.js `renderOrder`, queue minus 3000), as Unity draws; so the sea (2950, 2960) always goes before particles (3000) and the wind's ribbons (renderOrder 0), which no longer vanish behind it. Opaque meshes keep their order. The sky is drawn before everything (renderOrder -1000): it tests no depth, so it covers anything drawn before it (LeafOakMat's leaves, at 2902, were, 2026-10-31). Materials: each renderer's first material is read from its `.mat`, and drawn with the faces its shader culls (its first `Cull`, or the material property `Cull [_Prop]` names; Unity's default and its built-in shaders cull back faces; strips and LeafSplit leaves draw both sides, as the game does); its base color (Canopy/General's `_Tint`, else `_BaseColor` or `_Color`) tints it only if its shader reads that property, as the game would (BarkSimple and LeafCut never read the `_Color` their materials keep from older shaders; a see-through material still takes that color's alpha as its opacity); its color texture, normal map, and (for Canopy/General) detail texture are stored in `cache/textures/` at most `maxTexture` px (ImageMagick; TGA, TIF and PSD become PNG, their extra channel kept as alpha). Textures changed in the Canopy working tree but not yet committed are read from there. **Textures** switches back to plain gray. Needs `pkg install imagemagick`.

Sky and sea (the Sky and Sea prefabs, above; a scene without them has neither): the sky is Canopy's `CanSky.shader` on a dome around the camera, with the skybox's panorama (`Sky3j.jpg`, a 2:1 image Unity makes a cubemap; sky blue until it loads). As in the game, a sky point's height (the camera's, plus its direction's height times `skyRadius`) picks its color: black below `skyBlackMult` × camera height + `skyBlackAdd` (the sky darkens as you go deep), the fog color below camera + `skyBlendMin` (the horizon fades into the fog), blending to the panorama from camera + `skyBlendMax`, and above that the mood's tint (`skyTint`, `skyTintLevel`; stand-ins, the game varies them by mood and depth). The numbers are `Game.unity`'s EnvironmentManager (60, 300, -9, 1200), in `unity.json`'s environment. `skyRadius` is how far around the camera Unity draws the skybox: by default ten times `far` (4 km), the only distance at which the game's black line falls where the sea ends, so fog fills down to the sea's edge and the depths look black; set a number to override. Bigger thins the horizon band and brings the black up to the horizon. The sea is `Sea.prefab`'s, kept under the camera as the game keeps it: from above, black water with the sun's glints, its ripples drifting, clear looking down and opaque toward the horizon; from below, its dark blue underside. While the camera is above the sea, everything below it darkens as in the game. Composed scenes not on an area are in Unity's world too, so build them above it: New Assets and Divers Guild Hall stand at 150 m.

Surface shaders: a material whose shader has a surface function between `MODL SURFACE` markers (made by `modl/tools/shaders.js`, in `Assets/Shaders/Modl`) is drawn by `web/surface.js` with that same function as GLSL, its property values from the `.mat`, and Canopy/General's lighting and fog (the `light` settings), not Three.js's. A shader that fails to compile shows in red.

An imported prefab instance that changes objects inside its prefab (a rotated arm, a moved marker, a switched-off gem holder), at any depth of nesting, carries its own parts in the area file. Ids inside nested prefabs follow Unity's rule: instance id XOR source id. Of sibling LOD meshes, the most detailed one present is shown (some prefabs hold only a Lod3). Objects that scripts show or hide at run time (e.g. a Payee's amber payment marker) appear as they are saved, unless a `unity.json` rule says otherwise.

FBX models (binary FBX) are read by `tools/fbx.js`: each mesh is scaled as Unity imports it (the .meta's Scale Factor, times the file's unit when Convert Units is on; checked against a Unity-made LOD mesh, exact to 0.1%), with its geometric offset, and its origin moved to its pivot, as Unity does on import. The mesh an object uses is found by the name Unity listed; else by an older-style id (4300000, 4300002, …: the n-th mesh in Unity's reading order, depth first; checked against every model that lists its ids); else a newer-style id, the signed xxHash64 of `Type:Mesh-><name>0` (`tools/xxhash64.js`; checked on the BlackJar meshes); else the object's name; else the file's only (or only LOD0) mesh; else, as a guess, its largest. Binary FBX 6 (2006-era files such as `Bowl.FBX`: models named rather than numbered, each holding its own mesh) is read too. ASCII FBX is not read (a few store-pack models are ASCII). A model file placed directly in a prefab (the Driller's drill, the Dagger, the Booster) comes in whole, as one mesh in its root's space; `.obj` model files are read too (`tools/obj-model.js`, the meter as unit). Changes such a prefab makes to objects inside the model file are not applied; no game prefab makes any.

Material analysis (for `docs/Rendering Performance.md`): `node tools/materials.js` reports every material and shader by rendering cost: shaders in use with their flags (clip, blend, fog, fog level, darkening below the sea, Multiview stereo macros, texture reads), opaque work queued above 2500 (sorted back to front), weak and missing fog, unused shaders and materials, the largest textures; `node tools/materials.js <name>` gives one material's or shader's findings, `--json` all of them. The raw `.mat`, `.shader` and texture `.meta` texts are kept in `cache/canopy-sources.json`, so each is read or fetched once; a rerun takes seconds. Run `node tools/guid-index.js` first when Canopy changes.

Layer analysis: `node tools/layers.js` reports how each of Unity's 32 layers is used: objects on it in prefabs and scenes (with renderers, colliders, rigidbodies), prefab overrides that move objects onto it, which layers it collides with, the cameras and lights that leave layers out, how often live C# names it, and layer names the code uses that don't exist. `--json` gives all of it. Cached in `cache/layer-analysis.json`; a rerun takes seconds.

What ships: `node tools/uses.js` lists, by folder, the assets reachable from the build (the enabled scenes in EditorBuildSettings, Addressables groups and every Resources folder), following references through scenes, prefabs, materials, controllers and ScriptableObjects; `node tools/uses.js <path text>` says of each matching asset whether it is in use and by which chain of references; `--json` gives every in-use path. Assets loaded by name from code other than Resources aren't seen. Cached in `cache/uses-analysis.json`; a rerun takes seconds.

Branch tips: `node tools/tree-tips.js` reports the shipped tree's open branch tips by side count (found on the uncut `GridForestTreeCollider`, where a branch's buried base is told from its tip), the larger openings, and the matching tips in each `StdTreeLods` chunk by LOD; `--cap` closes them with flat fans (no vertex added; only the index buffer changes). `--source`, `--prefab`, `--scale`, `--max-sides`, `--max-radius` pick another tree. Meshes are read from the Canopy working tree when checked out, so a rerun shows what is left.

Leaf outlines: `node tools/leaf-fit.js` reports, for each leaf material in use, the share of its quad that is leaf at the material's cutoff, and of the tightest 4, 5, 6 and 8-cornered outlines that stay inside the texture; `<material>` picks materials, `--json` gives the outlines' UV corners.

Not yet: materials of a mesh's second and later submeshes. `cache/` can be deleted any time; it refills on demand (the Beech area's textures are about 32 MB capped, 155 MB at full size).

Leaf meshes: `node tools/leaves.js` reports, and `--write` writes into the Canopy working tree, six-cornered leaves (outlines from `leaf-fit.js`): per-texture `ForestTreeLeaves<Kind>_Lod0-2` meshes wired into the leaf prefabs and scene overrides, and each LeafSplit's `leafOutline` and `placementMesh`.

Re-chunking trees: `node tools/dice.js` rebuilds each LOD level of a gridded tree (`StdTreeLods` by default) whole from its chunks (undoing CanopyGrid's slicing: cut vertices on the grid planes are removed where their triangles lie in one plane, or two meeting in a straight crease) and cuts it again keeping triangles whole: a triangle goes to the cell holding its centre when it reaches no more than `--slack` (0.25 of a cell) past it; longer ones are cut, and their neighbours get the same edge points. It reports triangles before and after, area and open edges; `--candidate` writes the chunks to `cache/candidates/` for ☰ Candidate trees, `--write` into Canopy. `tools/mantis.js` is Mantis LOD's simplifier ported from CanopyGrid's managed plugin, for making LOD levels from a whole tree; `tools/unity-mesh.js` reads and writes Unity mesh assets with all their channels.

Building a tree from its source: `node tools/tree-build.js` makes a gridded tree's chunks from its source model, from the command line: the model as Unity imports it (`fbx.js` `modelMesh`: imported normals, welded vertices, 8-bit colors; tangents from `unity-mesh.js`, within a degree of Unity's MikkTSpace), its open branch tips closed once (`tree-tips.js`; each merged to a point, its vertices keeping their UVs so the bark runs to it; `--fans` caps all but the small or snub ones with flat fans, which smear the bark; the collider keeps its fans), each LOD level made by the Mantis port at its quality (`--qualities 100,50,25`, Dicer's for StdTreeLods), faces Mantis leaves twice dropped, each level cut into the prefab's chunks with whole triangles (`dice.js`), and branch strips (`--strips L1:lost,L2:all`): from the tree's skeleton (its branches' rings), segments as camera-facing strips of two triangles, written beside each chunk as `<chunk>.strips.asset`: in L1 the segments Mantis lost, L2 the whole tree (no mesh), drawn out to `--far` (400 m) since it is cheap. Layout draws strips with the bark material widened and shaded as round cylinders (`stripPatch` in `web/app.js`); Canopy has no strip shader yet, so strips are Layout-only until it does. It reports triangles against the shipped chunks, with area and open-edge checks; `--candidate` writes to `cache/candidates/` (☰ Candidate trees), `--write` into Canopy. Defaults: `--source Assets/Meshes/ForestTreeCollider.fbx --prefab Assets/Prefabs/Trees/StdTreeLods.prefab`.
