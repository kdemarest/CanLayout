# layout

View Unity areas and compose Canopy scenes on the phone, in the browser.

    runLayout            # starts it in the background and opens http://localhost:8080
    runLayout stop       # stops it;  runLayout log  follows its log

`runLayout` is a two-line launcher in `~/.local/bin` (on PATH) that runs `bash /sdcard/dev/layout/runLayout.sh "$@"`; scripts on the sdcard cannot run directly, only through `bash`. The launcher and the Termux:Widget button (`~/.shortcuts/runLayout`, same one line) live outside version control: `setup.sh` recreates both. Without them, run `bash /sdcard/dev/layout/runLayout.sh`.

While it runs, any edit to layout's files restarts the server once the files have stopped changing for 1.5 s (so edits in quick succession restart it once, with all of them), and the page reloads itself (or, with unsaved changes, shows Reload). `node server.js` runs it once, without the watcher. The server also watches the Canopy working tree's uncommitted files (those `cache/guid-index.json` marks local, and their folders and every folder above them, up to `Assets`), polling their times each second while a page is open: when one changes (say modl's `python3 obj/curved_bench.py --write` rewrites its benches), it updates the index (rehashing rewritten files in place, else rerunning `tools/guid-index.js`) and the page reloads its scene where the camera is, unless it has unsaved changes. It waits until the files have been quiet a second, so one write reloads once. It knows Tweak: mid-drag the reload waits for the finger to lift, and tweaks not yet written survive it and are put back on the rebuilt pieces. A tweak family's sources (its spec doc, script and library, as its worker names them) are watched too: an edit made outside Tweak, such as a tag changed in Obsidian or the script edited, rebuilds the family from the doc, and the scene reloads. Each request rereads the guid index if the file changed.

Assemblages: a scene whose frontmatter says `assemblage: true` becomes a prefab of its own, `Assets/Assemblages/<Name>/<Name>.prefab` (`tools/assemblage.js`; Name: the scene's name in PascalCase), so another scene places it as one piece, as Unity nests a prefab. Its root is the scene's `- Origin: x y z` line. A **linked** assemblage (`assemblage: linked`; `tools/linked.js`, Ken, 2026-11-09) is made of existing Canopy objects whose Unity data must survive (Black Isle, Hawthorn's outland): `node tools/linked.js migrate <Scene> <Group> "<Name>" --write` moves a group out of a Canopy scene into the prefab, fileIDs, overrides, children and Canopy uids kept, and writes its scene with one line per object carrying `link <fileID>` and `in <its nearest linked parent>`. The prefab stays the source of everything but placement: on save only moved objects' local placements change (worked out under their parents' new ones; an unmoved line rewrites nothing), a new line becomes a new instance, a line taken out deletes its object if nothing else refers to it; in the page, moving a linked piece moves the pieces `in` it. **Placing assemblages in Canopy's scenes** (Ken, 2026-11-09: a biome scene refers to each instance it wants, a Hut ten times or the Amphitheater once): open the biome's imported area, Add ▸ Scenes, pick the assemblage (it starts at its Origin when that is in sight, so a one-of-a-kind authored in place drops where it was designed), carry it and Drop: `tools/placements.js` writes a PrefabInstance of its prefab under the scene's anchor, patches the scene's areas and logs the change in docs' Assemblage Placements Changes (`docs/code/changes/`, linked from Canopy Changes); tap a placed one to carry it again (Drop moves it, Delete removes it); the rest of an area stays read-only. Extracts draw placements by name, from the assemblage's current prefab; editing an assemblage over its biome hides its own placements there. The server doesn't reload for scene files it wrote itself. Pieces whose prefab is a LODGroup over nested models (modl's benches) have those models nested directly under the root's one LODGroup: level k is every such piece's level k, and the last level is one mesh per material combining every piece's last level (`<Name>_Far.obj`), one draw where there were many; Unity's LODGroup only switches renderers and never combines them. Its levels end where the latest piece's own would, plus the assemblage's radius. Other pieces are nested whole, with their own LODs. The server rebuilds an assemblage when its scene is saved and when Canopy files outside `Assets/Assemblages` change (its pieces); only files whose text changed are written. In a scene with an Origin, Tweak keeps a piece its worker anchors on a ring (a curved bench at its row's radius) at its angle around the Origin and moves it to the ring's new radius; leaving Tweak saves the scene with them.

Inspection models: a tool can write OBJ files with a color (`cache/inspect/<Name>.obj` and `.json`: `{color, opacity, material, side}`) for a scene to place as `- Piece: inspect:<Name> | …`. They are drawn lit in their color, both sides unless `side` says `front` or `back` (a back-only copy of a front-only surface lines its inside), see-through below opacity 1 (one naming a Unity material by guid is drawn in that material, textured, while Textures is on), each its own ☰ Folders entry (`Inspect/<Name>`) so layers can be switched on and off. `node tools/mesh-clean.js <Assets/… mesh> [--inspect <Prefix> [--material <name>]] [--skip rejoin] [--write]` (`--material BarkBeech` for the Tangle: its Clean and Original layers textured) makes such layers for a branchy mesh (a tree's uncut collider, e.g. the Tangle's `Tangle_LOD0.mesh`): stray pieces (orphans), its trunk origin, each segment between consecutive rings classed straight, turned, unclear or self-crossing (an hourglass) and whether at the origin, a tip or elsewhere; it rejoins self-crossing segments straight across (`--write` saves the cleaned mesh into Canopy). `--texture-only` (with `--from <local file>` to read another tool's output) only textures every branch afresh, start to end, the cleaner's last step. `node tools/mesh-hub.js <mesh> --radius <units> [--ease <rings>] [--inspect <Prefix>] [--write | --out <file>] [--ball <Assets/... .asset>]` hides a tree's hub, the knot of branch starts at its trunk origin, in a ball, widening the trunk smoothly into it, and writes the ball as a mesh in GridTangle's vertex format. Each run also writes a change log, `cache/mesh-clean/<mesh>.jsonl`: per change, the rule, the branch, the rings, the triangles and why; `node tools/mesh-why.js [<mesh>] [x y z | --probe <n>] [--r <units>]` lists the changes near a point (mesh units; by default the last probe's, `--probe 2` the one before) and the branch and ring it's on; `--probes` lists the recent probes, numbered.

Conditions (Ken, 2026-10-29): Canopy switches objects on and off, and moves and scales them, by expressions over its progress flags (`SkyIsle`, `TangleDeposits>=4`, `!Shambles`), evaluated by `MathEval.cs`. ☰ World › **Conditions** cycles three ways to draw a scene: **met** (the default: every condition the game can meet, met), **start** (as fast-forwarding to the scene's stage starts it) and **saved** (as saved in Unity, conditions ignored). An object off in the mode is hidden; ☰ World › **Inactive** draws it faint instead, its scripts not running. When a scene or prefab is imported, `tools/conditions.js` finds what gates each object, and the gate goes on its piece or part (`when`: terms that must all hold; `never`: off whatever the flags; `moved`: where its triggers put it):
- `ActiveWhen`, `EffectActive` (and `EffectActiveMany`'s targets), and `EffectBoolActive` under an `EffectBoolTrigger`: on exactly while the condition holds.
- Triggers (`OnCondition`, `OnConditionOnce`, `OnConditionDoEffect`, `EffectTrigger`, `EffectConditional`) play the effects on their `effectsToExecute` object: an `EffectScale` or `EffectMove` whose target starts inactive leaves it off until the trigger's condition holds; an `EffectScale` to zero makes it gone then (the Sky Isle's cloud); `EffectMove`, `EffectTransform` and `EffectScale` to a size move it (`moved`); a Pod's `Revealer` (PodRequest) hides the pieces under its `revealRoot` until then.
- `HideOnStart`, a disabled renderer, and an object saved inactive that nothing switches on: never on in the game (the Sky Isle's guard Blowtorches, the four FruitLoci).
- A `GateMini`'s lamps (Ken, 2026-11-03: the spikes on the Approach's gate; `tools/unity.js` gateLamps): only its first `depositsRequired` Lamps under its `lampParent` show, unlit (their `lampOn` never), and only while the gate runs, in its own stage (`StageName="<stage>"`: its nearest stage filter's, FilterOnStage or FilterOnPopulate, else the scene's, and Tutorial in Game.unity), as `FindLamps` and the gate's dormant and complete states do. A scene may point `lampParent` at another instance (the Approach's BeechGate at its BeechMini): that piece's parts get the gates. Or at a plain object of the scene, the lamps pieces placed under it (Tangle's OakGate, its ForestMini's seven flower BadLamps; Hawthorn's LumaGate, its CloudMini's four cloud lamps; 2026-11-09: they showed in every stage): each such piece holding a Lamp is gated the same way. The order of the Lamps is the files' (Unity's is the hierarchy's), so which of them show may differ; how many does not.

A scene's symbols (each flag's value in met and start) come with it from the server: met has Game.unity's `FastForwardSymbols` for every stage, then every flag an `EffectSetProgressFlag` or `EffectMath` anywhere in Canopy sets (set to one: 1; incremented: how many do), and each stage's `<Stage>Deposits` at its Gates' most required; start has only the stages before the scene's. Both have `StageName` from the scene's name (`TangleScene`: Tangle), or from `unity.json`'s `stage` for a scene whose name is not a stage (an outland's working scene plays as its biome's, `partOf`: BlackIsleScene as Hawthorn's); there is no `Stage` number, in Canopy either (2026-11-09: conditions test stages by name). Stages are compared by the order they are played, `Stage.ValidStages` in Canopy's `StageId.cs` (Tutorial, Beech, Tangle, Hawthorn, Oak, Luma…), never by their numbers, which are permanent and say nothing of order (Hawthorn is 10); `tools/conditions.js` `stageOrder` reads it. Flags code sets at run time (`Underwater`, `RingsWorn`) are unset unless `unity.json` `symbols` sets them; a composed scene's `- Symbols: A=1 B=0` lines win. `web/conditions.js` is MathEval ported with its quirks (an unset flag is null, so even `X==0` fails until something sets X), and with Canopy's two fixes (2026-10-29: every waiting operator of equal or higher priority goes first, so `1-2*3+4` is -1; a `!` pops nothing, so `!!a` works). Like Canopy's MathEval it compiles each condition once (`compile`, `run`): the page evaluates programs. `Conditions.test()` runs MathEval's own test cases, both ways. The compiled design was proven here, against the interpreter on 340,000 random expressions and flag tables, before it went into Canopy. Not read yet: `FilterWithStageData` (which some ActiveWhens also test; no scene so far has one), an instance's override of a condition inside its prefab, and conditions on stand-in scripts (a gated particle system plays as saved).

Gems (Ken, 2026-10-29): a gem isn't saved in the scene; as each stage starts, its `GemLocus` makes it, so an imported area does too (`tools/gems.js`). `GemTypes` (`Prefabs/Shared/GemTypes.prefab`, with `Game.unity`'s added types) gives the locus's ability (`abilityId`, the scene's override) a shape and a color; the shape's gem prefab goes at the locus's `gemHolder`, keeping its own rotation (relative to the locus) and its own world size, as `Make.cs` places it; everything under its `model` takes the color's material, and its breadcrumbs (`BreadcrumbGemPfx`, the effect around a gem on its locus) the color's color. Its puisance shows only for a gem locked in place, and its glow and pick effects only on events, so they don't come. `tools/unity.js` reads `GemLocus`'s and `Gem`'s fields for this without running them (a graph's `read`).

Animation: `web/ticker.js` is the page's clock. Each frame the render loop calls `Ticker.tick(dt)` (seconds since the last frame, at most 0.1; 0 while ☰ **Tick** is off, which freezes everything animated) and it calls every function added with `Ticker.add((dt, time) => …)` (which returns a function that removes it); `time` is the sum of the dts, standing still while the page is hidden, and is also the shaders' `canopyTime`. A function that throws is dropped and its error shown. Scripts run on it.

Scripts (Ken, 2026-10-29): code attached to a piece, as a MonoBehaviour is to a Unity game object, run on the Ticker while the piece is in the scene: a class derived from `Tickable` (`web/tickable.js`, which says what each has: its piece, params, the camera, the environment, and for stand-ins their saved fields), in a file named for it that calls `Tickable.register("<Name>", class extends Tickable { start() {} tick(dt) {} stop() {} })`. They come two ways. Layout's own prefabs, `prefabs/<Name>.md`, for what Canopy lacks or layout runs its own way: `- Shape:` lines as a docs asset's (`use X.prefab` nests that Unity prefab, even where a layout prefab shares its name) and `- Script: <file> | settings` lines; a piece names one like any prefab, layout's winning over a Unity prefab of the same name, and they are never exported to Unity. And the C# scripts of Unity prefabs: one with a JS stand-in, `scripts/<ClassName>.js`, runs with its saved fields (`tools/unity.js` reads them, and the prefab's objects, which a stand-in may move); the rest are only named, and the probe report lists a piece's scripts (`(no stand-in)` for those). A prefab instance's overrides of a stand-in's settings (a C# script's fields, a particle system's and its renderer's properties) are applied through every level of nesting, as Unity does, except those naming objects (they would need their ids translated from file to file). So is a material an instance sets on a renderer inside it, in any slot, and a change to how many it has (since 2026-11-06, when it was found that 180 of Canopy's files set one: Resin's coral mesh draws in ResinMat, not Corals1). A script's params are its class's static ones, then its Script line's settings, then its piece's (`rate 2, speed 4`: numbers or lists of them). A class with `static singleton = true` runs once: a second piece with it is hidden and its scripts stopped (the first loaded wins, and a scene's included pieces load before its own). Editing a script, a stand-in or a layout prefab restarts the server, and the page reloads.

- **Sea** (`prefabs/Sea.md`): a cube nesting Canopy's own `Sea.prefab`, drawn and run as the game does: stand-ins for its `Sea.cs` (a singleton: the waterline's height and how things darken below it, from its `dimAmountDefault` and `dimDepth`), `Abyss.cs` (the prefab at the world's origin, its altitude from SeaCanonicalAltitude, 135, or a scene's `environment.seaLevel`, as a biome's local altitude; the water kept under the camera in its 14 by 12 m steps, moved only when looking steeply up from above the sea or down from below, or ten steps off) and `WaterScroller.cs` (each surface's two scrolls drifting by its `flow1` and `flow2`). Its two surfaces are drawn by their shaders' names (`web/surface.js` byShader, which also draws Canopy/Water, the bubbles' and the Mega's shield's, Canopy/Force Field, the cages' spheres, Canopy/CloudTextured, the clouds, each as its shader does, Canopy/DiffuseGlow, the mana fire on flamers (unlit, its bright texels blazing), and Canopy/Goop as its surface does, lit simply where Unity uses Standard lighting): Canopy/SeaSurface as the game's, on the mesh's own UVs and its material's `_Normal1` and `_Normal2` tilings; the underside as Canopy/WaterBelow, a cheap shader of Canopy's own since 2026-10-29 (ambient light on its color, a Fresnel sheen of the water's color, fog at `_FogLevel`; it was Unity's Standard lighting). The surfaces leave the piece for the world (so they aren't picked up, snapped to or framed); tap the cube.
- **Sky** (`prefabs/Sky.md`, `scripts/Skybox.js`, a singleton): CanSky on a dome round the camera, from the environment's `sky` and sky settings, as Game.unity's skybox with EnvironmentManager's sky globals. Without one the background is plain.
- **Sun** (`prefabs/Sun.md`, `scripts/Sun.js`, a singleton; Ken, 2026-11-01): the scene's one light, Canopy's sun from the environment's `sunDirection`, `sunColor` and `ambient`. Canopy's ported shaders take these anyway; the Sun lights Three.js's own materials the same way (a directional light and an ambient one, each pi strong, as Three.js's Lambert divides by pi), and turns off the page's plain lights (a hemisphere light and a fixed sun), which light scenes without one.
- **Wind** (`prefabs/Wind.md`, `pfx/Wind.js`, a singleton; an experiment, Canopy has no wind yet): very light tan lines wafting along +X, their front 15% (by length, `headFade`) fading in from near transparent and rounded, widening from nothing as a quarter ellipse, drawn with eight points of its own (`nosePoints`), so the leading edge is soft (2026-10-30), each a chain of points whose head alone moves, weaving gently (at most 0.2 m up or down per meter), laying points that stay put while the tail eats the oldest; each starts to the -X side of the camera within 3 m of its height (never lower than 3 m over the sea), placed to cross the view, and is gone in about ten seconds; it blows in gusts, lines starting for ten seconds, then none for five to ten.
- **Spinner** (`scripts/Spinner.js`, for Canopy's `Spinner.cs`): turns its `partToSpin` (else its own object) about its axis once every `secondsToSpin`, from a random start, as `Transform.Rotate` does. `Spinner.spin` is switched as the game switches it: a Daisy's saw (`scripts/Daisy.js`, with `scripts/HcRange.js`) spins only while the camera has woken it, within `awakeRange` until beyond `sleepRange`, and nothing spins in the Foyer. `scripts/HcSpinner.js` is `HcSpinner.cs` (the Blazer, AmberSpin): always on, about its own up. Cairn's spinners start when its portal is purchased (`EffectEnable`s on its `Payee`'s `OnPurchase` object, which `effectsOnPurchase` plays with `SetPaid`); layout treats a Cairn as paid (Ken, 2026-11-07), so a Spinner an `EffectEnable` turns on spins from the start (`tools/unity.js`). Stand-ins move and turn a prefab's objects with `offset` and `turn` (`web/tickable.js`). The docs scene Harvesters shows every harvester, spinners spinning. A stand-in script on a mesh-less object a scene hangs under a prefab instance joins that instance's scripts (Tangle's Dagger spins by a child named Spinner).
- **Lights** (`web/lights.js`; More Lights phase 4, Claude, 2026-11-08): Canopy's own point and spot lights, as Canopy's `CanopyLights.cs` lights the game. Unity's point and spot Lights in prefabs and scenes (`tools/unity.js` `lightOf`: color, intensity, range, culling mask, a spot's angles and its direction through nested prefabs; switched-off ones and those lighting nothing left out; a scene's plain object with a Light and no mesh is a piece that is only that light, drawn as a small ball of its color) are sorted Canopy's way (Ken, 2026-11-08: Unity's point lights, sorted our own way; spots too): the 31 nearest the camera (by how far past its range each is), and each mesh lit as Canopy/General is (General, GeneralCutout, GlowLit, TransparentLit) gets the four that light it most at its sphere (as the game: its bounds' center, the radius halfway between the sphere inside them and the one around them), only lights whose culling mask has its part's layer (the ForLighting layer's dedicated lights light only it). Each is lit as the sun is (N·L, the highlight), by its color made linear times its intensity, a spot within its cone (full inside the inner angle, none outside the outer; ranked by the part of its cone a mesh's sphere reaches), falling off as Unity's do (1 / (1 + 25 x), x the distance over the range squared, 0 at the range). As in the game, a LOD group's levels are one object with one list, measured over all its levels (Ken, 2026-11-08: they occupy the same space), and lists are kept for levels not showing, so a level that switches in is already lit. Spread out like the game's: lights chosen four times a second, each keeping its slot while chosen; 250 meshes' lists a frame; positions every frame; no fading, unlike the game. ☰ Light › Lights switches them (the game's `_CanopyLightCount`), ☰ Light › Light crowding colors each lit mesh by how many lights reach it (gray none, blue 1, green 2, yellow 3, red 4). WebGL 1 draws none. Surface-shader ports (`web/surface.js`: amber, goop, rim) don't take them yet.
- **Flamer and HcFaceTarget** (`scripts/Flamer.js`, `scripts/HcFaceTarget.js`): a flamer (Flamer, Blowtorch) with no target, as the game shows it: HcFaceTarget turns the head toward Flamer's aim, at most `rotationSpeed` a second, and with no target that aim is `straightUp`, which rides on the flamer's Spinner, so the head sweeps round; only within `scanRange` (40 m) of the camera. Stand-ins can also aim an object (`orient`) and read where a moved one is now (`positionOf`, `rotationOf`).
- **Particle systems** (`scripts/ParticleSystem.js`, from `tools/particles.js`, which reads a ParticleSystem and its renderer into a small description; Ken, 2026-10-29): Unity's Shuriken systems that play on awake, after the overrides of every prefab instance they're in (the rest wait for game events layout doesn't run, and aren't sent; HarvesterJet, in every flamer, turns its steam's off, so only a douse plays it): emission (rate and bursts) from the shape (cone, sphere, hemisphere, box, circle, edge), start lifetime, speed, size, rotation and color, then size, rotation, color and velocity over each particle's life, gravity (Canopy's physics gravity, 3 m/s², ProjectSettings/DynamicsManager.asset, times the system's gravityModifier; it was Unity's default 9.81 until 2026-11-09, so the many systems that rise by a negative modifier rose 3.3 times too fast), scaled as Unity's `scalingMode` says (since 2026-11-09: hierarchy scales the particles' size, where they start and, in local space, how they move by the emitter's world scale; local by its own scale only, never its parents'; shape only where they start; until then every system took only its piece's scale, so the 850 hierarchy-scaled ones under a cage's 0.25 and the like were drawn up to 4 times too big and moved too far), noise, local or world space, a texture sheet, drawn as camera-facing billboards in one batch by a port of the material's shader (Canopy/ParticleContrast, ParticleGeneral, ParticleSolid, ParticleUnlit, TransparentLit; Canopy/Blank draws nothing; others as texture times color; Unity's Default-Particle as a soft dot). Particle colors reach the shader as Unity hands them: converted to linear (Canopy draws in linear) only where the renderer's Apply Active Color Space is on; most of Canopy's have it off, so their colors go unconverted and look stronger (2026-10-30: the gems' green showed white). The page blends in linear as Unity does (Ken, 2026-11-03: the orange skybeams looked white): the scene is drawn into an sRGB buffer smoothed with 4 samples, so what is see-through adds up in linear, then copied to the screen (WebGL 1: straight to the screen, unsmoothed). Noise (2026-10-29: the gems' breadcrumbs wander) moves particles as a velocity from a gradient noise field (Ken Perlin's improved noise) at each particle's position: each axis its own field, sampled at the position times the frequency, moved along by the scroll speed, octaves added, in 1D, 2D or 3D by its quality, times the axis's strength over the particle's life (divided by the frequency with damping, as Unity's docs describe it) and the position amount; its remap and its effect on rotation and size aren't done (Canopy uses neither). Unity's noise function isn't public, so the field differs from the game's in detail, not in strength or scale. Trails (Ken, 2026-11-03: the sky beams above cages, rings, gems, runestones, the Temple and outposts are trails alone, their renderer's mode None, so no billboards are drawn) are strips facing the camera, all of a system's in one more batch with its renderer's second material (the trail material) through the same shader ports: per particle (a ratio of the particles each leaves a point every `minVertexDistance`, each point lasting the trail's lifetime times the particle's; its trail stays after it dies unless Die With Particles), or ribbons (Ribbon mode: `ribbonCount` strips through the particles in the order they were made, every nth in each, newest at the head); in the world's space or the emitter's; width over the trail (from its head, by length) times the particle's size, color over the particle's life and over the trail times its color; the texture stretched along it, tiled by length, or spread or repeated per segment. A beam's look (narrow, fading in over its first meters) is its texture's (LightningTrail: a thin bright line, its alpha from its brightness, dark at its ends). At most 4000 points a system; a cage's 15 systems cost about 4 ms a frame more on the phone. Modules it doesn't do (collision, stretched billboards...) are named once each in the console. As in the game, a system in a LODGroup level runs and shows only while that level is drawn (the page's LOD pass keeps each piece's chosen levels for scripts), and **PfxLod** (`scripts/PfxLod.js`) thins its emission with the camera's distance every 5 s and pauses it below 0.4 a second; off screen (the box round its particles, as of its last update, out of the camera's view) it does what its culling mode says, as Unity does: Automatic pauses a looping system and keeps simulating any other, Pause pauses, Pause and Catch-up catches up the missed time (up to its duration) when seen again, Always Simulate keeps simulating unseen (in the Tangle 273 of 276 pause); and a system more than 100 m from the camera waits. The Driller's smoke (DrillerSmoke) was the first.
- **Cage** (`scripts/Cage.js`, for Canopy's `Cage.cs`): its sphere drawn in `liveMat` while anything guards it, else `deadMat`, as `AdjustMat` does. Guards are counted when a Canopy scene is imported: each active instance whose `thingGuarded` (HcGuard's) names the cage, as HcGuard registers when the scene starts; the piece says `guards N`.

The docs scene World holds them (and LeafGust); `unity.json`'s `include` puts it in every scene from Canopy (an imported area, or a scene on one), and any other scene takes it with `- Include: World`. A scene without a Sea has no sea and nothing darkens; without a Sky, no sky.

Effects (pfx): `web/pfx.js`'s `Pfx` is a Tickable that is a tiny particle system whose effects are code: `pfx/<Name>.js` registers a class derived from it, with `spawn(p)`, `update(p, dt)` and `remove(p)` (and `setup`, `dispose`), each optional (`web/pfx.js` says what each gets). Particles spawn at `rate` a second, at most `max` at once, each living `life` seconds unless `update` ends it sooner. An effect works in Unity coordinates: its piece's own (scaled with it), or with `static space = "world"` the world's, the piece only switching it on. It has `space` (the group for its meshes), `eye` (the camera, in those coordinates), `inView(near, far)` (a random point the camera sees) and `ribbon(n, options)` (a strip of n points facing the camera, a line with thickness, set each frame with a width and an alpha per point). A layout prefab's Script line attaches it.

A LODGroup may name renderers inside nested prefabs or models (stripped renderers), as modl's prefabs do (a LODGroup over nested `<Name>_LOD<k>.obj` models): those models' meshes take its levels.

The reticle button (right of ⛶) probes: a ray from the camera through the screen's center; the first visible piece it hits is marked with a yellow dot and reported on the status line, and the server keeps the full report in `cache/probes.jsonl` (newest last) and logs it: the piece, its model, object, material and LOD levels, the point in Unity world and piece-local coordinates, the surface normal, the distance, the hit triangle's corners and the 6 vertices nearest the hit (piece-local, with their distance in metres), and the camera's place and direction, and its scripts, if it has any. On a `mesh-clean.js` layer (its `.json` names its `source` mesh) the server adds, a few seconds later, `branch`: the branch and ring the point is on and the changes near it (`tools/mesh-why.js --json`). Tell Claude "what about this?" and it reads the latest. Holding the button 1.2 s goes back to where the latest probe was taken (from any page), facing the same way, and shows its dot.

☰ Inspect layers (shown when the scene has inspection models) lists each layer with its color and an on/off box, leaving out layers with nothing in them (say, no orphans left). Both lists scroll when they outgrow the screen. A scene line `- Switch: Before = A B | After = C` adds a top-bar button naming the set of inspection layers now shown; a tap shows the next set alone (its layers on, the other sets' off), for a before and after in the same place (docs scene Compare Tangle: `mesh-clean.js` writes `<Prefix>Changed`, every triangle it changes, red, to lay over Original). What ☰ Folders and ☰ Inspect layers switch off is kept per scene in the browser between runs.

☰ Origin shows a scene's `- Origin` (an assemblage's root) as axes drawn through everything: red Unity +X, green +Y, blue +Z (0 degrees); it is greyed out in scenes without one.

## Two kinds of scene

- **Composed scenes** are yours: `docs/scenes/<Type> <Name>.md` (e.g. `Landmark Ring Forge.md`), one Piece line per piece. Create one with ☰ **New**; edit it here or by hand. They are what will be exported to Unity. A line `- Include: <Scene>` draws another composed scene's pieces beneath, read-only, their scripts running (World: the sea, sky and wind); included scenes' own includes come too, each scene once, and their Area lines are ignored. Include lines aren't exported.
- **Imported areas** are read-only copies of a cube of a Unity scene named for the biome or place they hold (Ken, 2026-11-09): Tutorial, Beech, Tangle, Hawthorn, Oak, Luma, Mushroom, Maple, Coral and Umbra are all of their scenes (TutorialScene and so on), each extracted risen: a scene saved in storage (Mushroom, Maple, Coral and Umbra are at -1000) is lifted as its rise lifts it when its stage starts (RiseControl's `altitudeSpan.max`; the area's `source.risen` says by how much), since ☰ Fast forward always snaps and hides biomes not risen yet; nothing Unity tags EditorOnly, or anything under it, is extracted, as the game leaves it out (Ken, 2026-11-09; Game.unity's Build group is tagged so), every piece with its own LODs from Canopy (extracted with center 0 0 0 and half 1000000); Game is all of Game.unity's World (`--under World`: its places, not its player rig, editor objects or managers): the Approach, the Temple with Sky Lance Isle, its moon cannon and Idol Island (World/BeechBiome, which rises with Beech), the Stronghold (World/MoonBiome) and every biome's region. The game always has Game.unity loaded, so every Canopy scene draws Game alongside (`unity.json` defaults `with`; Ken, 2026-11-09: as safe and consistent with the game as can be); Tangle Terrain (TangleScene's trees only, Terrain only) is a work in progress, not a biome and not a Canopy scene: it is to be diced (`tools/dice.js`) to replace the Tangle's existing cut-up tree (Ken, 2026-11-09; docs/tasks/General.md). The list shows them in the order they are played (`server.js` `areaOrder`: Game first, then a stage's name, an outland's `unity.json` `title`), the rest after. ☰ **Import area** (or `node tools/scene.js extract <Scene> <cx> <cy> <cz> <halfSize> <Name> [--terrain] [--under <path>]`; Game is `extract Game 0 0 0 1000000 Game --under World`) writes `areas/<Name>.json`; name it for what it holds. Regenerate them whenever Unity changes. Its Terrain only box brings just the scene's trees, none of its gameplay pieces (e.g. the Tangle's GridTangle alone). Trees (already cut into cubic chunks, `Meshes/Grid*`) come in as one read-only piece per tree holding only its chunks whose bounds overlap the cube, every LOD level of each (see LODs). ## unity.json

What an imported area shows is set in `unity.json`, not in code: `defaults`, plus a section per scene under `scenes` (its values replace the defaults; its show and hide lists add to them).

- `near`, `far`: the camera's clip planes in meters, the game's by default (0.03 and 400: `Game.unity`'s OVRCameraRig camera, which both eyes use; `FarClipManager` may pull far in at run time, to 40 m in clouds). Nothing past `far` is drawn, as in the game; raise it to see farther while composing.
- `gridDrawsInactiveParts` (true): draw a GridRender tree's chunks even though its `Parts` object is inactive, as GridRender does (scenes save it inactive; GridRender skips only disabled renderers). `gridLodByDistance` (true): pick GridRender chunk levels by distance, as Canopy's GridRender does since 2026-10-25 (it measured a vertical segment's screen height, which shrinks seen from above); false shows the old way.
- `leafThinning`: LeafSplit's thinning to preview where a scene's LeafSplit has none: `{ "start": 40, "end": 200, "keep": 0.3, "grow": true }` (meters, the share left at `end`, grow the rest); `keep` 1 is off.
- `environment.leafWind`: LeafSplit's leaves in the wind, Canopy's `iLeafCutWind` shader's settings with its defaults, to tune here and then set on the material: `{ "sway": 0.075, "flutter": 0.01875, "frequency": 1.5, "gustLength": 12, "gustSpeed": 4, "gustShape": 2, "near": 0.25, "nearDistance": 8, "fadeDistance": 1000 }`. Blown, each leaf turns rigidly about its stem toward its own axis plus `sway` of downwind (1 turns a leaf across the wind 45°) and `flutter` of its face's direction, back and forth `frequency` times a second, at wind strength 1 (medium), the steady wind and gusts added. The wave gusts (☰ World › Gusts as waves) are waves `gustLength` m apart moving downwind at `gustSpeed` m/s, lulling to stillness between them (the wave from 0 to 1 raised to `gustShape`: higher, shorter gusts and longer calms). Only `near` of the motion at the eye, all of it from `nearDistance` m (calm close up, against VR sickness); none beyond `fadeDistance` m (full to half of it). Only the fields given change. Drawn in the vertex shader (`web/app.js` windPatch), as the game's shader does; the stem and tip come from the leaf's uv.y (0 and 1), as LeafSplit places its leaves.
- The wind itself is LeafGust's (Ken, 2026-10-31): World's LeafGust piece (`prefabs/LeafGust.md`, run by `scripts/LeafGust.js`, the stand-in for Canopy's `LeafGust.cs`). A steady wind everywhere plus gusts, each a level, added, never past strong: ☰ World › **Wind** cycles the steady wind (still, light, medium, strong: strengths 0, 0.4, 0.7, 1) and **Gusts** the gusts (none, light, medium, strong: 0, 0.4, 1, 1.7); **Gusts as** switches cylinders and waves; remembered per scene. Cylinder gusts: up to three round columns of wind at once, never more, each passing through a point in front of the camera (`nearest` 10 to `farthest` 60 m away, within `viewAngle` 40° of where it faces), starting `approach` (25 m) upwind of it and ending as far downwind, building up and dying out, so it is strongest there; heading along `direction` (+X, a little +Z) give or take `directionJitter` (15°); `radius` 25 m (light 15, strong 35), easing off over the last `edge` (4 m), at `speed` 4 m/s; the next at least `interval` (4 s, give or take `intervalJitter`, half) later and in a free slot. Light gusts are smaller, slower (0.75) and rarer (1.4 times the interval); strong ones bigger, faster (1.5) and more often (0.7). A leaf whose stem is in a gust (tested by squared distance: no square roots) is blown along its heading; it takes the strongest it's in. Settings on World's LeafGust line change these (e.g. `radius 30, interval 6`). The Wind effect's lines follow it (`pfx/Wind.js`): a few for the steady wind, along it, and more inside each gust, travelling with it.
- `lodFov`: the headset's vertical field of view per eye, in degrees, used to pick LOD levels as the game would (90 for now; to be measured in the game, `Camera.fieldOfView` under XR). `lodBias`: Unity's LOD bias (QualitySettings; Android's level has 1). `treeChunks`: path pattern of chunk meshes (`Meshes/Grid*`).
- `maxTexture`: largest texture side kept, in pixels (1024), for PNGs: the page's fallback, and tools such as `material-preview.js`. The page draws textures compressed where the phone's GPU reads ASTC: `tools/texture-astc.js` makes an `.astcm` beside each cached PNG, at the size the game draws it (the `.meta`'s Android size when its override is on, else the default platform's; at most 4096), from the source when the PNG is smaller (its own mipmaps; 6x6 blocks for opaque colors, 4x4 for normal maps and anything whose alpha is read, the alpha dropped where the material's shader ignores it, as on General's opaque materials; astcenc is built by `setup.sh`). The server runs it in the background after a load, so a new texture is a PNG until the next load; `node tools/texture-astc.js --all` does the whole cache at once. `layout.log`'s `load:` line from the page says how many of each and their GPU memory. A texture's alpha is what Unity's import makes it (its `.meta`'s Alpha Source, which `tools/guid-index.js` notes): None drops it, From Gray Scale makes it the average of R, G and B (the gems' glow, `VFX_Glow.png`, is an RGB image whose black ground is clear that way), and Input Texture Alpha keeps its own (TIF and PSD extra channels count as alpha). Converted textures are cached by source, size and alpha source. The page decodes each texture from sRGB or not as Unity samples it, by its own import setting (a Normal Map never; else its sRGB box, older metas' `linearTexture`), whatever slot it fills. A Normal Map made on import from a gray height map (Create from Grayscale: the bouncers' `Mushroom_a_bump.png`, three coral textures) is made the same way here (`storeNormalFromGray` in `tools/scene.js`: forward differences for Smooth, an unnormalized Sobel filter for Sharp, times Bumpiness), cached by its settings; Unity's exact formula isn't published, so its strength is a guess until calibrated against Unity. `server.js` serves textures by a pattern of their names, so a new suffix on a cached name must be added there too. The page also makes material colors linear from the sRGB the `.mat` keeps, as Unity does in Canopy's Linear color space, before lighting in linear as Unity does.
- `show`: rules forcing objects on even when the scene or prefab switches them off (the tree leaves: `mesh:ForestTreeLeaves*`).
- `hide`: rules removing objects.
- `symbols`: progress flags code sets at run time, for ☰ World › Conditions (see Conditions): `{ "Underwater": 0 }`.
- `include`: the composed scenes every scene from Canopy includes, as an `- Include:` line would (an imported area, or a scene on one): `["World"]`.
- `environment`: the sun and fog Canopy's shaders get (`ShaderVars.cs`): `sunDirection` (Unity world, toward the sun), `sunColor`, `ambient` (Canopy's ambient intensity, 0 to 1), `fogColor`, `fogDensity` (the game's least is 0.014, `fogDensityMinimum` in `Game.unity`); the sky, `sky` (a material name: `CanSky`, the skybox) and its settings (below); and `seaLevel`, null where the Sea prefab's own altitude stands (135), set where a scene's biome sets its own (TutorialScene's 200.48). The sea is the Sea prefab's (its values are `Sea.prefab`'s). With `live` (on unless set false) these stand only until the game's light is worked out from where the camera is (Light, below). ☰ **Fog** turns the fog off.

A rule is `kind:pattern`, `*` matching anything: `prefab:Cairn` (a whole piece), `prefab:Cairn/PlaceholderObject` (an object inside it), `object:`, `mesh:`, `material:`, `folder:Prefabs/PFX`. Example: `"scenes": {"BeechScene": {"hide": ["folder:Prefabs/PFX"]}}`. An area is re-imported automatically the next time it is opened after a `unity.json` change to what it bakes in: `show`, `hide`, `treeChunks`, `gridDrawsInactiveParts`, `gridLodByDistance` and `leafThinning`, as they come out for its Unity scene; or when a stand-in (`scripts/`) is added or removed, since an area keeps its pieces' stand-in scripts (their saved fields; editing one needs no re-import); or when how prefabs are read changes (`tools/unity.js` READER). The rest (clip planes, culling, LOD, environment, include and so on) is read each time a scene loads and needs no re-import.

A composed scene can be built on an imported area: a line `- Area: <Name>` (e.g. `- Area: Tangle`) draws that area beneath it, read-only; several Area lines draw several, the first giving the Unity scene's settings (fog, environment, culling, LOD). ☰ **Areas** draws other areas alongside whatever is open, read-only, remembered per scene in this browser (e.g. Beech while viewing Hawthorn: Unity's scenes share one world, so they line up); the scene's own areas are ticked and fixed, and a change reloads the scene, keeping the camera (save first). Each area's pieces keep their own scene's stage for Conditions; the rest of the settings are the open scene's. New on an area brings the areas drawn alongside as Area lines. Its pieces can't be picked up, but Snap rests pieces on them, and Folders and Labels cover them. Save writes only the scene's own Piece lines; export will take only those. While viewing an area, **Add** (or ☰ **New**) asks for a scene name, makes the scene on that area, and keeps the camera where it is.

The Add list has three sections: new assets from docs (◇ drawn from shapes, ▢ a labeled box), reused assets by docs name (`Tablet → RuinTablet`, from 🟢 Asset lines; placed as the prefab), and every Unity prefab, so any Unity object can be placed.

Placeholder shapes: a new asset with `- Shape:` lines indented under its docs Asset line is drawn from them (◇ in the Add list) instead of a labeled box (▢). Shapes: `box w h d`, `cylinder r h`, `cone r h`, `pyramid w h d` (a w by d base), `sphere r`, `hemisphere r` (flat side down), `dodecahedron size`, `octahedron size` (point to point, a point up; `octahedron 0.5 scale 1 2 1` is 1 m tall), `icosahedron size`, `ico60 size` (a triakis icosahedron: an icosahedron with a low pyramid on each face, 60 faces; point to point, an edge up, so `ico60 0.6` is 0.51 m tall), `prism w h d` (a triangle w wide and h tall, extruded d thick), `use <Name>` (another asset's shapes, or a Unity prefab, e.g. `use Wood boards at 0 1 0`); then optionally `at x y z`, `turn rx ry rz` (degrees), `color #rrggbb`, `material <Name>` (a Unity material by file name, one word, e.g. `material ModlMana`; drawn with it here and used by the placeholder prefab; the color stays as a fallback), `sides N` (cylinder, cone), `tip f` (cone, pyramid: cut off flat, the top f times the base, a snub nose), `scale x y z`, `arc degrees` (hemisphere: keep a wedge, e.g. `arc 90`); `use <Name> color #rrggbb` recolors all of it. Meters, Unity axes; each shape rests on its origin. Bad lines show as errors. Reload the page after editing them.

Unity names: a docs asset's prefab is its name in PascalCase (Burl shard -> `BurlShard`), or the name after `as` on its Asset line (`- Asset: 🟢 Model: Command Aerie as Lookout`); `as LeafSingle.fbx` names a model file, placed whole (also in `use`). When that prefab exists in the project, layout draws it instead of the shapes.

Moving and copying in Canopy's scenes (Claude, 2026-11-09): `node tools/move-object.js <rules.json> [--write]` moves objects with everything under them from one scene into an object of another, every fileID kept, at the same world placement (the scenes' rises allowed for); copies an object into another scene with new fileIDs (stripped ones as Unity makes them), its names, overrides (an instance's, by its name) and uids (a Gate's: its name's) set, the scripts named left out; renames objects; and sets the stage filters under an object. The header of the file has the rules' form; cache/move/summoners.json is the summoners' after the Hawthorn split.

## LODs

Layout draws LOD levels as the game picks them, every frame, as seen by a camera with the headset's field of view (`lodFov`) from where Layout's camera is:

- **LODGroups** (prefabs' and nested prefabs' own, in areas and composed scenes): Unity's rule. The group's size (times its object's scale) over its distance from the camera, as a share of the view's height (size / (2 · distance · tan(fov/2)), times `lodBias`); the first level whose screen height that reaches is drawn, else nothing.
- **Trees a GridRender draws** (`StdTreeLods`: Beech, Oak, Maple, the tutorial tree; the Tangle, whose scene adds one): Canopy's `GridRender.TestLoDs`. Per chunk: if the box of all its level meshes is in view (as Canopy's GridRender does since 2026-10-26; it tested a cube `objectSize` times the tree's scale across at the chunk's part, which missed geometry the slicer left outside it, so branches vanished while their part's centre was behind the player), the cube's size over its distance as a share of the view's height, in percent (`gridLodByDistance`; before, the screen height between its bottom and top centers, which shrinks seen from above); the first of GridGroup's `cutoffArray` it reaches is drawn, else nothing. The chunks' own LODGroups are ignored, as in the game, where GridRender switches them off.
- **Other gridded trees** (the mushroom): their chunks' LODGroups, resized as `GridGroup.AdjustCutoffs` does at startup (size `objectSize`, heights the cutoffs when it has them).
- A tree's leaves use their own LODGroup (`TreeLeavesLods`), except where the scene adds a LeafSplit to the tree (the Beech, Oak and Maple scenes): the game then draws every leaf itself, instanced, out to its `maxRenderDistance` (250 m), so the most detailed leaf mesh comes, cut to the area leaf by leaf, and is drawn as LeafSplit would, with its thinning: farther leaves drop out a share at a time (each leaf has a fixed random rank, the same hash as `LeafSplit.LeafRank`; at distance d a leaf shows while its rank is under keep(d), from 1 at `start` to `keep` at `end`), the rest grown by 1/√keep with `grow`; a leaf shrinks to nothing over the last `fade` (5%) of ranks, and every leaf over the last 10% of the distance limit, instead of blinking out. The scene's LeafSplit settings are used when it has thinning on (`thinKeep` under 1); else `unity.json`'s `leafThinning` previews it (`keep` 1 turns the preview off). The piece's settings line says which.
- **Candidate trees**: chunk meshes a tool wrote to `cache/candidates/` (the same paths as in Canopy; `node tools/dice.js --candidate`) are paired with the shipped ones when a scene loads; ☰ **Candidate trees** (shown when there are any) switches every tree between the two, with the same LODs, so ☰ **LOD tint**'s triangle count compares them. A candidate tree's own GridRender cutoffs (`cache/candidates/<prefab>.lod.json`, from `tree-build.js`) apply with it. Nothing goes into Canopy until a tool's `--write`.
- Not yet: LODGroups on plain scene objects (outside prefabs); those show their most detailed level.

## Composing

- **Add** opens a filterable list (new assets from docs, then Unity prefabs). The piece appears on the cursor a few meters ahead, sized to about a quarter of the screen.
- Drive the camera like a player with the WASD pad (W toward where the camera points, S straight back, A and D sidestep, Q down, E up), or the same keys on a keyboard. One finger looks around (carrying a piece, a sideways drag circles the camera around it instead); a pinch travels forward and back like W and S. over 2 s of holding, every move key (W, S, A, D, Q, E) speeds up from a sixtieth of the speed (44 m/s, `MOVE_SPEED`, the same in every scene; carrying, 0.75 of the piece's size) to six times it, A and D at half that; a pinch goes farther the faster the fingers move (four times 0.2 + (speed / 400 px a second)^1.5, that factor at most 12), so slow pinches stay fine.
- While a piece is carried it keeps its place relative to the camera, so moving carries it. ## How it gets meshes

The browser keeps every file the server sends (meshes, textures, scripts, the page), checking each time: a file comes with an ETag of its size and modification time, and one unchanged since comes back as a 304, without being read again (2026-10-31: Oak's 1345 meshes and textures, 89 MB, in 1.1 s instead of 2.8 s). A rebuilt or edited file is sent again.

The server resolves each piece name through its Unity prefab (nested prefabs and variants followed; every LOD level a LODGroup draws, else the most detailed of sibling LOD meshes; only meshes with an enabled renderer), fetches the `.asset` meshes it needs from the Canopy git repo (through a throwaway store, `~/.cache/layout-fetch-<pid>.git`, one per process, deleted after each fetch, so Canopy's own git store stays lean; blobs Canopy already has are read from it), and converts them to OBJ in `cache/meshes/`. `node tools/guid-index.js` rebuilds `cache/guid-index.json` after the Canopy repo changes, including uncommitted changes in the working tree (new, changed, renamed or deleted assets), whose files are then read from the working tree. The committed part is kept per commit (`cache/guid-index-head.json`), so a rerun on the same commit only rereads the working tree.

Draw order follows Unity's render queues (2026-10-30): each material's queue is its own (`m_CustomRenderQueue`) or else its shader's `Queue` tag (`Transparent+1` and the like), and a mesh or particle system with a queue past 2500 is drawn in queue order before distance (three.js `renderOrder`, queue minus 3000), as Unity draws; so the sea (2950, 2960) always goes before particles (3000) and the wind's ribbons (renderOrder 0), which no longer vanish behind it. Opaque meshes keep their order. The sky is drawn before everything (renderOrder -1000): it tests no depth, so it covers anything drawn before it (LeafOakMat's leaves, at 2902, were, 2026-10-31). Materials: each of a renderer's materials is read from its `.mat`, and drawn with the faces its shader culls (its first `Cull`, or the material property `Cull [_Prop]` names; Unity's default and its built-in shaders cull back faces; strips and LeafSplit leaves draw both sides, as the game does); its base color (Canopy/General's `_Tint`, else `_BaseColor` or `_Color`) tints it only if its shader reads that property, as the game would (BarkSimple and LeafCut never read the `_Color` their materials keep from older shaders; a see-through material still takes that color's alpha as its opacity); its color texture, normal map, and (for Canopy/General) detail texture are stored in `cache/textures/` at most `maxTexture` px (ImageMagick; TGA, TIF and PSD become PNG, their extra channel kept as alpha). Textures changed in the Canopy working tree but not yet committed are read from there. **Textures** switches back to plain gray. Needs `pkg install imagemagick`.

Light (Claude, 2026-11-03): the sun, ambient, fog and sky are worked out each frame from where the camera is, as the game's EnvironmentManager does (`web/environment.js`, from what `tools/moods.js` reads out of Canopy; `node tools/moods.js [Scene…]` lists it). The zones are every BiomeZone in the game's scenes (`Chunk.cs`'s list and the scenes being drawn), at any depth of nesting, with their instances' overrides: each tree prefab carries one (Beech's and the Temple's mood is MoodForest, Oak's and Hawthorn's MoodOak), and so do Game.unity (the Temple, the Moon, the moon beam), the Tutorial, the Tangle, Luma, Mushroom, Maple and Hawthorn's graveyard. The dominant zone is the active one (as saved; stage filters aren't applied) the camera is most within (BiomeZone.GetPercentWithin: full inside its falloff margin, 10%, easing to nothing at its edge), else Game.unity's default zone; its MoodVisual, or under the sea the Sea's, gives the fog's color and density by depth (the camera's height between the mood's `yBottom` and `yTop`; under the sea, its depth over the Sea's `maxDepth`), never thinner than Fog's `fogDensityMinimum`, and by light (depth, with no overcast here) the sun's intensity, the ambient, the sky's tint, its tint level by depth, the sun's color and angle where the mood sets them (else the Sun light's in Game.unity), its sky material and the sea's dimming. Each eases toward its value as `Perp.Towards` does, over the mood's (or Fog's, 4 s) `fogLerpSpeed`; the fog snaps crossing the waterline, everything on a load or a jump of over 50 m. Curves and gradients are Unity's (Hermite keys, weighted ones as Bezier; gradients blended or fixed); `Environment.test()` checks them. Canopy's shaders take the sun's direction, its color times its intensity (`canopySunLight`, Canopy's `_SunLight`, which every lit Canopy shader takes since 2026-11-06; its color made linear, as ShaderVars makes it), the ambient and the fog (`web/surface.js`); the Sun stand-in lights Three.js's materials with the same. Not yet: clouds', goop's, ink's, poison's and death's fog, rain's overcast.

Sky and sea (the Sky and Sea prefabs, above; a scene without them has neither): the sky is Canopy's `CanSky.shader` on a dome around the camera, with the skybox's panorama (`Sky3j.jpg`, a 2:1 image Unity makes a cubemap; sky blue until it loads). As in the game, a sky point's height (the camera's, plus its direction's height times `skyRadius`) picks its color: black below `skyBlackMult` × camera height + `skyBlackAdd` (the sky darkens as you go deep), the fog color below camera + `skyBlendMin` (the horizon fades into the fog), blending to the panorama from camera + `skyBlendMax`, and above that the mood's tint (`skyTint`, `skyTintLevel`, live from the mood: Light, below). The numbers are `Game.unity`'s EnvironmentManager (60, 300, -9, 1200), read from it while live, else `unity.json`'s environment's. `skyRadius` is how far around the camera Unity draws the skybox: by default ten times `far` (4 km), the only distance at which the game's black line falls where the sea ends, so fog fills down to the sea's edge and the depths look black; set a number to override. Bigger thins the horizon band and brings the black up to the horizon. The sea is `Sea.prefab`'s, kept under the camera as the game keeps it: from above, black water with the sun's glints, its ripples drifting, clear looking down and opaque toward the horizon; from below, its dark blue underside. While the camera is above the sea, everything below it darkens as in the game. Composed scenes not on an area are in Unity's world too, so build them above it: New Assets and Divers Guild Hall stand at 150 m.

Canopy/General is lit as the game lights it (2026-11-05; docs: Rendering Performance, More Lights, phase 1): its own formula in place of Three.js's (`generalLight` in `web/app.js`, a patch on the material's shader): ambient times the color, plus the sun (its color times its intensity) times N·L times the color, plus its Blinn highlight (8th to 64th power by `_Glossiness`, a fifth of the sun times gloss), the sun and ambient from Canopy's globals as surface shaders take them, the color as bright as `_Tint` times `_Brightness` makes it, over 1 if so. So General pieces warm and dim with a mood's sun, as everything lit does (2026-11-06; before, General took neither). Other materials keep Three.js's standard material under the scene's lights.

Surface shaders: a material whose shader has a surface function between `MODL SURFACE` markers (made by `modl/tools/shaders.js`, in `Assets/Shaders/Modl`) is drawn by `web/surface.js` with that same function as GLSL, its property values from the `.mat`, and Canopy/General's lighting and fog (the `light` settings), not Three.js's. A shader that fails to compile shows in red.

An imported prefab instance that changes objects inside its prefab (a rotated arm, a moved marker, a switched-off gem holder), at any depth of nesting, carries its own parts in the area file. Ids inside nested prefabs follow Unity's rule: instance id XOR source id. Older scenes (TangleScene, Game, Beech, Luma, Foyer) gave stripped objects small ids instead, so a stripped object's source is read from its `m_CorrespondingSourceObject` (Claude, 2026-11-08: the Foyer's rings, the Vacancy payment slots and Beech's MachinePart21 had hung from their instance's root). An object parented to an object inside a nested prefab (not its root) hangs from that object, with its placement and scale (NoteLore's stone hangs from its NoteBase's paper, which flattens it to the tablet; Black Isle's Thrusters hang inside RuinShaman), in prefabs, scenes and splits alike; and components an instance removes (`m_RemovedComponents`) are gone (NoteLore removes the paper's own cube) (Claude, 2026-11-03). Of sibling LOD meshes, the most detailed one present is shown (some prefabs hold only a Lod3). Objects that scripts show or hide at run time (e.g. a Payee's amber payment marker) appear as they are saved, unless a `unity.json` rule says otherwise.

FBX models (binary FBX) are read by `tools/fbx.js`: each mesh is scaled as Unity imports it (the .meta's Scale Factor, times the file's unit when Convert Units is on; checked against a Unity-made LOD mesh, exact to 0.1%), with its geometric offset, and its origin moved to its pivot, as Unity does on import. The mesh an object uses is found by the name Unity listed; else by an older-style id (4300000, 4300002, …: the n-th mesh in Unity's reading order, depth first; checked against every model that lists its ids); else a newer-style id, the signed xxHash64 of `Type:Mesh-><name>0` (`tools/xxhash64.js`; checked on the BlackJar meshes); else the object's name; else the file's only (or only LOD0) mesh; else, as a guess, its largest. Binary FBX 6 (2006-era files such as `Bowl.FBX`: models named rather than numbered, each holding its own mesh) is read too. ASCII FBX is not read (a few store-pack models are ASCII). A model file placed directly in a prefab (the Driller's drill, the Dagger, the Booster) comes in whole, as one mesh in its root's space; `.obj` model files are read too (`tools/obj-model.js`, the meter as unit). Changes such a prefab makes to objects inside the model file are not applied; no game prefab makes any.

Material analysis (for `docs/code/Rendering Performance.md`): `node tools/materials.js` reports every material and shader by rendering cost: shaders in use with their flags (clip, blend, fog, fog level, darkening below the sea, Multiview stereo macros, texture reads), opaque work queued above 2500 (sorted back to front), weak and missing fog, unused shaders and materials, the largest textures; `node tools/materials.js <name>` gives one material's or shader's findings, `--json` all of them, and `--unity` the shaders the game draws that don't light the Canopy way (Unity's built-in shaders, surface shaders and others lit by Unity's lights, shaders from packages outside `Assets/Shaders`), each drawn material with where it is drawn, then per shader the materials used by prefabs or scenes but never drawn, to move onto Canopy's own (docs: Rendering Performance, More Lights). The raw `.mat`, `.shader` and texture `.meta` texts are kept in `cache/canopy-sources.json`, so each is read or fetched once; a rerun takes seconds. Run `node tools/guid-index.js` first when Canopy changes.

Shader check (Claude, 2026-11-06; docs: Rendering Performance, More Lights): `node tools/shader-check.js [name…] [--all] [--force]` compiles every shader in Canopy's `Assets/Shaders` (`--all`: the UNUSED_ ones too) as Unity would for the Quest, so mistakes show before Unity is opened: each CGPROGRAM's vertex and fragment programs, in each keyword variant, through glslangValidator as HLSL, against the include files of Canopy's own Unity version (its builtin_shaders zip, downloaded once into `cache/unity-builtin/`) with the OpenGL ES 3 defines and the includes Unity adds. Errors name the `.shader`'s lines. Surface shaders and HLSLPROGRAM blocks are listed, not compiled: every Unity shader is being replaced by Canopy's own (Ken, 2026-11-06). Passes are cached by what was compiled (`cache/shader-check.json`) and compiles use every core: a full run is about 25 s, a rerun only what changed. It is not Unity's own compiler: it catches what HLSL rejects (syntax, types, undefined names), not Unity's limits. Exits 1 on any failure.

Page shaders without a browser: `node tools/page-glsl-check.js` compiles the page's own lighting patches (`web/app.js` `generalLight` for Canopy/General and GeneralCutout, `glowLitPatch` for GlowLit, on three.js's standard material) and the particle shader ports (`scripts/ParticleSystem.js`) with glslangValidator, as WebGL2 gets them, so a typo shows before the page is opened; the shaders go to `cache/page-glsl/`. The page lights Canopy/General, GeneralCutout (its own 8th-power highlight), GlowLit (with its thresholded glow, `_GlowMode` and constant glow; the game's glow fade taken as 1) and TransparentLit (blended by its alpha) as the game does; particles on ParticleUnlit (Unity's Particles/Standard Unlit's port: color and alpha modes, additive by its `_DstBlend`) and TransparentLit (lit smoke) by ports in `scripts/ParticleSystem.js`; RimLit by `web/surface.js` (additive, its inner color lit by `_InnerLit`); Canopy/Diamond is still drawn as a plain material (its cube maps aren't read).

Moving materials off Unity's shaders (Claude, 2026-11-06; docs: Rendering Performance, More Lights, phase 3): `node tools/convert-materials.js <Name>… [--write]` (names are `.mat` file names, `*` for any run: `Corals*`) prints what each material becomes: Standard opaque and the Mobile and Legacy diffuse shaders onto Canopy/General, Standard cutout onto GeneralCutout (`_Color` as `_Tint`, `_BumpMap` as `_NormalMap` at `_MainTex`'s tiling, `_BumpScale` as `_NormalStrength`, smoothness as `_Glossiness`, `_Brightness` 1), Standard with emission onto GlowLit (its constant glow, `_EmissionColor` × `_EmissionMap`), Standard's Fade and Transparent and lit particles onto TransparentLit, Particles/Standard Surface's opaque and cutout onto ParticleSolid, Particles/Standard Unlit and Mobile/Particles/Additive onto ParticleUnlit, Canopy's glow surface shaders (GeneralGlowUsingDetailTwo, GeneralGlowUsingDetail, GlowSurface, NoFogEmissiveLight) onto GlowLit, Oculus's Hands_Transparent onto RimLit (`_InnerLit` 1) and FX/Diamond onto Canopy/Diamond (the file's header lists each mapping), with warnings where Canopy's shader can't match (metallic, Standard's ×2 detail, a smoothness or occlusion map, a specular color, soft particles), and why it leaves a material alone. `--rewritten <Shader file>… [--write]` is for Canopy's own shaders rewritten in place (Goo, ParticleSolid, Blank), whose materials don't change: the shader as it was (git HEAD) goes to `ShaderSwitch/Shaders/` as `Canopy/Unity/<name>`, and each material on it gets a copy on that, so the switch back works for them too. `--write` converts in place (same guid, so prefabs, scenes and Edit/Build's LOD prefabs draw it untouched; Unity's properties stay in the `.mat`, unused; CRLF files stay CRLF), copies each as it was to Canopy's `Assets/Resources/ShaderSwitch/<Name> (Unity).mat`, lists the pair in `ShaderSwitch.asset` (read by Canopy's `ShaderSwitch.cs`, the game's switch back), and reruns `tools/guid-index.js`. New files' guids are md5 of `canopy-shader-switch:` and their path, so converting again after an undo writes the same ones. `node tools/material-preview.js <Piece>… [--yaw 30] [--pitch 20] [--size 360] [--sun 1] [--ambient 0.2] [--normal 1] [--highlight 0.2] [--texture 512] [--out file]` draws each piece's LOD0 twice, side by side, to `/sdcard/Download/preview.png`: on the left as Unity's shader draws it (a converted material's copy; Standard as Unity's BRDF1: GGX, Smith visibility, Disney diffuse, Fresnel, the ambient standing in for its reflection probe; Mobile and Legacy diffuse as Lambert; Mobile/Bumped Specular as its Blinn-Phong; Canopy's glow surface shaders as Standard with their glow), on the right as Canopy's draws it (exactly `CanopyLighting.cginc`'s math; for a material not yet converted, what `convert-materials.js` would make of it), under the same light (Game.unity's sun angle and color, a flat ambient), with emission and the thresholded glows (the game's glow fade taken as 1), and each side's mean brightness printed; a part on a shader it can't draw is left out and named. It rasterizes in software (orthographic, depth-tested, back faces culled by their normals, drawn at twice the size and halved), with the meshes and textures `tools/scene.js prepare` fetches. The page's ☰ Surfaces › **Unity's shaders** (shown when the scene has a converted material) draws those materials as their copies again, which the server sends with them (`unity`); Unity's Standard is drawn with its own smoothness (Three.js's roughness is 1 − smoothness, GGX like Unity's) and metallic.

Layer analysis: `node tools/layers.js` reports how each of Unity's 32 layers is used: objects on it in prefabs and scenes (with renderers, colliders, rigidbodies), prefab overrides that move objects onto it, which layers it collides with, the cameras and lights that leave layers out, how often live C# names it, and layer names the code uses that don't exist. `--json` gives all of it. Cached in `cache/layer-analysis.json`; a rerun takes seconds.

What ships: `node tools/uses.js` lists, by folder, the assets reachable from the build (the enabled scenes in EditorBuildSettings, Addressables groups and every Resources folder), following references through scenes, prefabs, materials, controllers and ScriptableObjects; `node tools/uses.js <path text>` says of each matching asset whether it is in use and by which chain of references; `--json` gives every in-use path. Assets loaded by name from code other than Resources aren't seen. `--live` (with any of them) counts only what the game can show or run (Ken, 2026-11-06: things placed only under inactive objects or a test or unused heading aren't in use): a scene's or prefab's references made only by pieces that never turn on (`tools/unity.js`: inactive with nothing to switch them on, HideOnStart, a disabled renderer), or by anything under an inactive object whose pieces all never show or under an object whose name has "test" or "unused" in it, are dropped before following; scripts on live objects still count, so a prefab a live spawner names is followed. Its `drawnMaterials()` says where the game draws each material: the live pieces of the build scenes (instances' material settings applied), the live parts of every prefab a live script names or a Resources folder holds, particle renderers, and materials a script names; `materials.js --unity` uses it. Cached in `cache/uses-analysis.json`: each scene's and prefab's live answer and drawn materials under a hash of the files it reaches but materials (nested prefabs, scripts, models, ScriptableObjects), so a rerun takes seconds, also after converting materials; a changed prefab redoes the scenes and prefabs that reach it, and a change to what `--live` reads (`LIVE_VERSION`) everything, about three minutes.

Branch tips: `node tools/tree-tips.js` reports the shipped tree's open branch tips by side count (found on the uncut `GridForestTreeCollider`, where a branch's buried base is told from its tip), the larger openings, and the matching tips in each `StdTreeLods` chunk by LOD; `--cap` closes them with flat fans (no vertex added; only the index buffer changes). `--source`, `--prefab`, `--scale`, `--max-sides`, `--max-radius` pick another tree. Meshes are read from the Canopy working tree when checked out, so a rerun shows what is left.

Leaf outlines: `node tools/leaf-fit.js` reports, for each leaf material in use, the share of its quad that is leaf at the material's cutoff, and of the tightest 4, 5, 6 and 8-cornered outlines that stay inside the texture; `<material>` picks materials, `--json` gives the outlines' UV corners.

Amber (Ken, 2026-11-07): `Canopy/Amber`, opaque but seeming translucent, is `web/surface.js`'s AMBER_FRAGMENT and `tools/material-preview.js`'s `amberLit`, the same math as `Assets/Shaders/Amber.shader`. An imported area keeps its prefabs' parts, mesh blobs included: after rewiring a prefab, bump `READER` in `tools/unity.js`, by a write that changes the file's time, or areas keep the old mesh. `Canopy/AmberShell`, the translucent skin of Ken's layered amber over a dark core, is AMBER_SHELL_FRAGMENT and `amberShellLit`; `tools/material-preview.js` draws what blends in a second pass over the rest. The page blends it in sRGB and the game in linear, so the two differ a little. The docs scene Model Amber shows the modl family (`AmberModel`, for Tweak) beside the game's Amber and `AmberLayered`.

The obsidian dagger (Ken, 2026-11-07): `node tools/dagger-split.js [--seed 7] [--flakes 9] [--slope 0.5] [--keep 0.6] [--gloss 0.985] [--highlight 2] [--tint 0.08] [--hilt-normal 3]` writes it into Canopy in two materials: one mesh from GridObsidianDagger's two cut pieces, the normal map with scallops baked over the blade (the lower envelope of a bowl per flake, seeded along the mesh's real edges, each texel through its tangents, over the map's own fractures), both materials, and GearDagger and Dagger unpacked to draw them. Reading scenes and prefabs (Ken, 2026-11-07: are the re-imports suboptimal?): shared storage costs about a millisecond a file call, and a scene's import was mostly that, OakScene reading 593 files 3547 times and asking after stand-ins 18824 times (57% of its 21 s). `tools/unity.js` now keeps each prefab as parsed, lists `scripts/` once, and keeps the last two scenes read, all forgotten when the guid index changes, as its prefab cache is. All the areas re-import in about 19 s, from 135 s, with the same result. The server holds about 200 MB more for it.

Several materials on one mesh (Claude, 2026-11-07; RuinPillar's middle was black, drawn in its first material's atlas): as in Unity, a mesh stays one mesh, its submeshes ranges of its triangles, and a renderer's material slot i draws submesh i. A submesh with no slot isn't drawn; a slot past the last submesh draws that one again. The converted OBJ keeps its faces in submesh order and says their sizes (`# submeshes 2046 1842 754`; an FBX's are one per material its polygons use, in material order, which its prefabs' slots were checked against), and the page makes them three.js groups on the one `THREE.Mesh`, with a list of materials. A piece's own `material` setting draws the whole mesh in it. A script's material change (`useMaterial`, as `sharedMaterial`) and a gem's replace slot 0 only. Probe reports the hit triangle's material and `submesh`, and tapping selects that material for Tweak. `cache/` can be deleted any time; it refills on demand (the Beech area's textures are about 32 MB capped, 155 MB at full size).

Leaf meshes: `node tools/leaves.js` reports, and `--write` writes into the Canopy working tree, six-cornered leaves (outlines from `leaf-fit.js`): per-texture `ForestTreeLeaves<Kind>_Lod0-2` meshes wired into the leaf prefabs and scene overrides, and each LeafSplit's `leafOutline` and `placementMesh`.

Re-chunking trees: `node tools/dice.js` rebuilds each LOD level of a gridded tree (`StdTreeLods` by default) whole from its chunks (undoing CanopyGrid's slicing: cut vertices on the grid planes are removed where their triangles lie in one plane, or two meeting in a straight crease) and cuts it again keeping triangles whole: a triangle goes to the cell holding its centre when it reaches no more than `--slack` (0.25 of a cell) past it; longer ones are cut, and their neighbours get the same edge points. It reports triangles before and after, area and open edges; `--candidate` writes the chunks to `cache/candidates/` for ☰ Candidate trees, `--write` into Canopy. `tools/mantis.js` is Mantis LOD's simplifier ported from CanopyGrid's managed plugin, for making LOD levels from a whole tree; `tools/unity-mesh.js` reads and writes Unity mesh assets with all their channels.

Building a tree from its source: `node tools/tree-build.js` makes a gridded tree's chunks from its source model, from the command line: the model as Unity imports it (`fbx.js` `modelMesh`: imported normals, welded vertices, 8-bit colors; tangents from `unity-mesh.js`, within a degree of Unity's MikkTSpace), its open branch tips closed once (`tree-tips.js`; each merged to a point, its vertices keeping their UVs so the bark runs to it; `--fans` caps all but the small or snub ones with flat fans, which smear the bark; the collider keeps its fans), each LOD level made by the Mantis port at its quality (`--qualities 100,50,25`, Dicer's for StdTreeLods), faces Mantis leaves twice dropped, each level cut into the prefab's chunks with whole triangles (`dice.js`), and branch strips (`--strips L1:lost,L2:all`): from the tree's skeleton (its branches' rings), segments as camera-facing strips of two triangles, written beside each chunk as `<chunk>.strips.asset`: in L1 the segments Mantis lost, L2 the whole tree (no mesh), drawn out to `--far` (400 m) since it is cheap. Layout draws strips with the bark material widened and shaded as round cylinders (`stripPatch` in `web/app.js`); Canopy has no strip shader yet, so strips are Layout-only until it does. It reports triangles against the shipped chunks, with area and open-edge checks; `--candidate` writes to `cache/candidates/` (☰ Candidate trees), `--write` into Canopy. Defaults: `--source Assets/Meshes/ForestTreeCollider.fbx --prefab Assets/Prefabs/Trees/StdTreeLods.prefab`.

Splitting a scene: `node tools/split-scene.js <rules.json>` cuts a Unity scene in two by its anchor's groups (the root object whose children hold everything), as a rules file in `splits/` says: the scene, its anchor, the new scene and its anchor's name, the groups that go to the new scene, and pieces that cross the line, each moved with everything under it into a group on the other side (one there, or one the rules `create`, empty, at its parent's origin), its world placement kept. Both scenes keep the scene's settings; every object keeps its fileID; a material saved in the scene goes to each half that uses it. Before writing, it checks that neither half refers to an object in the other (Unity can't keep such links), that every object is placed in the world where it was, and that every piece keeps its activity and game conditions. Without `--write` the halves go to `cache/split/`; with it, into Canopy (logged in `docs/code/Canopy Changes.md`); `--from <file>` splits a copy taken before an earlier write. A created group with `from` and `to` (world points) is placed: made at `to`, with what moves into it keeping its place relative to `from`, so it all moves by to - from, unturned (the checks expect that); `componentsOf` gives it copies of another object's scripts, all or those named (a FilterOnPopulate or StageFilter, so its pieces keep their stage). `tracker` gives the new scene the root every game scene has beside its anchor, a ChunkTracker naming the Region its chunk belongs to (Chunk.GetRegion), and `bareAnchor` keeps only the new anchor's transform (Game.unity's World has a World script). A scene setting Game.unity has (LightingSettings) goes in both. `splits/HawthornScene.json` (2026-11-02) made HawthornScene from OakScene; `splits/BlackIsleScene.json` (2026-11-03) took Black Isle out of Oak's top to where Ken probed.
